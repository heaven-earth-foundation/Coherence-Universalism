# Preface: A Constitution for an Open Framework

A foundational document should make a framework easier to question, not harder.

Coherence Universalism seeks relations among physical structure, living organization, perspective, meaning, social coordination, ethics, and artificial intelligence. Those relations become credible only when the framework distinguishes definitions from discoveries, mathematical results from physical hypotheses, empirical generalizations from bridge claims, normative commitments from descriptive facts, and spiritual interpretation from scientific proof.

This document is therefore a constitution rather than a catalogue of certainties. It states the vocabulary shared across the series, the limits on cross-domain translation, the principles governing consequential application, and the rules by which the framework may lose, revise, or retire a claim. Domain-specific questions remain with their owning papers. A principle's authority identifies the formulation to cite; it does not imply that the claim has been proven beyond its stated status.

The governing maxim is:

> **Preserve the integrative vision by claiming no more integration than the argument, evidence, and affected communities can support.**

# How to Read This Document

Each principle contains, where relevant:

1. **Authority** — whether the principle governs the framework, a domain, an application, or an interpretation;
2. **Claim status** — definition, formal result, conditional result, empirical claim, bridge, hypothesis, interpretation, commitment, or open question;
3. **Statement** — the controlling wording;
4. **Scope** — what the principle does and does not say;
5. **Operational consequence** — what an application must do;
6. **Failure modes** — recurrent misreadings or misuse;
7. **Review gate** — the expertise or participation required before consequential use or later revision.

A reference to coherence without a domain, constraints, relational structure, scale or access map, and horizon is presumptively incomplete. An identifier supports navigation; it never replaces the ordinary-language statement of the principle.

# Status Vocabulary

Authority class and claim status are separate.

- **Framework principle:** controls shared definitions, methods, or boundary rules across the series.
- **Framework conditional principle:** controls only under declared assumptions or model classes.
- **Framework governance rule:** governs consequential use but is not a descriptive law of nature.
- **Domain authority:** the controlling CU formulation within one domain.
- **Domain authority—conditional:** the controlling formulation with explicitly conditional empirical or bridge content.
- **Provisional domain principle:** a retained hypothesis or construct not yet promoted to broader authority.
- **Domain governance rule:** an application requirement owned by a domain.
- **Diagnostic failure mode:** a named pattern of failure rather than a positive law.
- **Interpretive principle:** a metaphysical or spiritual reading compatible with, but not entailed by, the descriptive framework.
- **Commitment principle:** an official CU orientation constrained by facts, consequences, standing, and revision.
- **Historical alias or retired claim:** preserved for traceability but not active as independent authority.

Canonical inclusion means “this is the formulation CU presently proposes.” It does not mean “external review is complete,” “the empirical claim is proven,” or “consequential application is authorized.”

# Part I — Foundational Principles

## CU-FP1 — Coherence Under Constraint

**Registry status:** CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and bounded [Bridge principle]

### Statement

Coherence is **integrated relational organization under specified constraints**.

For any substantive domain \(\mathcal D\), a coherence assessment has the dependency form

\[
C_{\mathcal D}=C_{\mathcal D}(x;K,G,\pi,T),
\]

where \(x\) is the state, trajectory, or organization; \(K\) the relevant constraints; \(G\) the relational or coupling structure; \(\pi\) the scale, partition, observation, or access map; and \(T\) the temporal horizon.

### Scope

CU-FP1 defines a shared structural question. It does not assert that coherence is a substance, field, force, cosmic purpose, or single measurable quantity. It does not imply that everything is harmonious or morally good.

The bounded transcendental claim is that robust uses of identity, organization, persistence, meaning, or justification presuppose some relations and constraints by which the relevant object can be distinguished and tracked. This does not derive a unique ontology.

### Operational consequence

Every domain paper must specify the arguments hidden by any shorthand \(C(x)\). Where no defensible measure exists, the paper must use a qualitative profile, partial order, viability region, or explicit open problem rather than inventing a scalar.

### Failure modes

- treating coherence as an invisible substance;
- defining it as whatever succeeds;
- equating it with harmony, order, consistency, or agreement;
- using the word without specifying the system boundary;
- treating the definition as evidence that the universe is fundamentally a coherence field.

## CU-FP2 — Gradation, Profiles, Typed Scalars, Direction, and Incomparability

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and methodological constraint

### Statement

Coherence assessments may admit degrees, directions of change, thresholds, vector profiles, preorders, partial orderings, viable regions, Pareto frontiers, risk functionals, or trajectory properties, but none of these structures is assumed universally.

A domain may use:

- a measurement scalar for one declared construct;
- a vector profile \(\mathbf C\);
- a decision scalarization for one declared choice;
- a risk functional over uncertain states or trajectories;
- a preorder or partial order \(\preceq_C\);
- a viable set \(V\);
- a threshold family;
- a Pareto frontier;
- or a trajectory property.

Scalars are local, typed, purpose-bound, and scope-limited. A measurement scalar requires an independently defended interpretation of what it measures. A decision scalarization is a rule for choosing under declared tradeoffs, not evidence that the dimensions possess one natural total ordering. A risk functional additionally encodes a treatment of uncertainty, tails, delay, and irreversibility.

Protected conditions are screened before scoring. No scalar may compensate for a floor violation. Variation above a protected floor may enter post-screen comparison only when its measurement, comparability, authority, and decision relevance are independently justified; otherwise the coordinate remains lexicographic or outside scoring.

### Scope

Not every system lies on one line from less coherent to more coherent. Two organizations can remain incomparable because they trade robustness, flexibility, accuracy, integration, agency, welfare, distribution, reversibility, or long-horizon resilience differently.

Coordinate-level measurement does not require collapse of the whole profile. A validated measure of repair latency, regulatory capacity, information integrity, reversibility, or another coordinate may be useful while the larger profile remains only partially ordered.

A scalar validated for one construct, population, option set, institution, horizon, or domain does not transfer beyond that scope without a new argument. A family of local scalars may not be assembled by semantic drift into one universal coherence scalar.

### Operational consequence

Every scalar must declare its type.

A **measurement scalar** must state:

- the construct measured;
- units or scale interpretation;
- observation and error model;
- reliability and validity evidence;
- population and domain of validation;
- uncertainty;
- and any decomposition or invariance conditions.

A **decision scalarization** must state:

- why aggregation is required;
- normalization;
- weights or trade rates;
- who has authority to set them;
- protected floors and the screening rule;
- affected scope and distribution;
- temporal horizon;
- tie convention;
- decision purpose;
- and sensitivity to reasonable alternative specifications.

A **risk functional** must additionally state:

- the probability or uncertainty model;
- the loss interpretation;
- treatment of tail events;
- discounting or horizon treatment;
- treatment of severe or irreversible harm;
- and robustness to model uncertainty.

Where profiles are non-dominating, validation is insufficient, or the tradeoffs are not defensibly commensurable for the declared purpose, incomparability or underdetermination must be preserved.

### Failure modes

- binary coherent/incoherent labeling where the relevant structure is graded or plural;
- assuming a metric, geometry, gradient, or optimum before defining and defending it;
- presenting a decision rule as a discovered natural ordering;
- hiding protected conditions inside weights;
- allowing aggregate gains to compensate a floor violation;
- treating every above-floor difference as automatically tradable;
- treating a local score increase as global progress;
- transferring a scalar beyond its validation or authorization domain;
- and rebuilding a universal coherence scalar from purpose-bound instruments.

### Review gate

Corpus-wide conceptual review; measurement, decision-theoretic, statistical, ethical, and affected-community review proportionate to the scalar's purpose and reach.

CU-M2; CU-M4; CU-M6; CU-M10; CU-M11; CU-M13; CU-G1; CU-G2; CU-G5; CU-G7.

## CU-FP3 — Generative Constraint

**Registry status:** CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and [Bridge principle]

### Statement

Constraints do not merely remove possibilities. Under suitable organization, restricting a space of states or transitions can create stable relations, capacities, meanings, and forms of agency that would not otherwise exist.

### Scope

CU distinguishes restrictive, generative, constitutive, instrumental, viability-bearing, protected, and epistemic constraints. Their roles must not be inferred from mathematical form alone.

Constraint without integration can produce rigidity or noise. Integration without external constraint can produce fantasy, confabulation, or insulation.

### Operational consequence

For each important constraint, identify:

- who or what imposes it;
- what possibilities it removes;
- what capacities it enables;
- whether it is revisable;
- whose viability it protects;
- and whose agency it may restrict.

### Failure modes

- treating all constraint as oppression;
- treating all order-producing constraints as legitimate;
- mistaking an externally supplied AI loss function for an intrinsic stake;
- ignoring coercion because the resulting system appears stable.

## CU-FP4 — Multi-Scale and Multi-Horizon Evaluation

**Registry status:** CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological principle]

### Statement

A coherence claim is relative to scale and horizon. Apparent improvement within one boundary is not a global improvement when it depends on unmodeled damage exported to another system, scale, population, or future.

### Scope

“Local” and “global” are analysis-relative. The containing system for one question may be a subsystem for another. No finite analysis captures every consequence, but the boundary and omissions must be disclosed.

### Operational consequence

Claims of progress must identify:

- the local system;
- coupled systems;
- immediate and delayed effects;
- externalized costs;
- protected agents or capacities;
- and the horizon over which evaluation is made.

### Failure modes

- aggregate success purchased by local destruction;
- short-term relief that entrenches long-term fragility;
- institutional stability maintained through moral injury;
- AI optimization that degrades the human environment supplying its objectives;
- rhetoric that labels the preferred scale “global” by definition.

## CU-FP6 — The Accessibility–Entropy Relation

**Registry status:** CANONICAL–CONDITIONAL  
**Claim status:** [Formal result] in the finite classical setting; [Bridge principle] elsewhere

### Statement

For a specified finite partition, block averaging preserves coarse block masses while erasing within-block distinctions. Under that operation, statistical differentiation from global uniformity weakly decreases and Shannon entropy weakly increases. The losses and gains have the same equality condition but are not generally equal quantities.

Let \(u_N=(1/N,\ldots,1/N)\), define \(D_N(p)=\lVert p-u_N
Vert_2^2\), let \(B_b\) be blocks of size \(m_b\), let \(q_b=\sum_{i\in B_b}p_i\), and, when \(q_b>0\), let \(r_i^{(b)}=p_i/q_b\) for \(i\in B_b\). Blocks with \(q_b=0\) contribute zero to the weighted KL sum. Define

\[
[E_\pi(p)]_i=\frac{q_b}{m_b}
\quad(i\in B_b).
\]

Then

\[
D_N(p)-D_N(E_\pi p)
=
\sum_b\sum_{i\in B_b}
\left(p_i-\frac{q_b}{m_b}\right)^2,
\]

and

\[
S(E_\pi p)-S(p)
=
\sum_bq_b\,
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r^{(b)}\Vert u_{m_b})
\geq0.
\]

### Scope

This is an operation-specific information-theoretic theorem. It does not identify entropy with hidden coherence. It does not show that descriptive coarse-graining physically destroys information. It does not equate Shannon, thermodynamic, von Neumann, conditional, or horizon entropy.

### Operational consequence

Every entropy claim must state:

- the entropy notion;
- state space;
- partition or reduction;
- observer or subsystem;
- and whether the operation is descriptive reconstruction or physical dynamics.

### Failure modes

- “entropy is inaccessible coherence” as an identity;
- calling conditional entropy hidden differentiation;
- inferring physical production from observer ignorance;
- using the theorem as a universal First Law of psychology, society, or ethics.

## CU-FP7 — Failure, Collapse, Adaptation, and Repair

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL as a schema  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and [Bridge principle]

### Statement

A domain-specific failure occurs when organization required by an explicitly declared criterion can no longer be maintained, adapted, or repaired within the relevant horizon and intervention class.

CU distinguishes perturbation, degradation, drift, fragmentation, threshold crossing, collapse, compensation, accommodation, transformation, restitution, restoration of standing, non-recurrence, and repair.

### Scope

Collapse is not one universal mathematical event. Repair is not synonymous with restoration, symptom relief, renewed coordination, forgiveness, reconciliation, or return to a prior baseline. Some losses are irreversible; truthful repair may require changed identity, boundaries, restitution, accommodation, or preservation of memory.

### Operational consequence

A failure or repair claim must state:

- the protected functions or standing;
- the affected parties;
- the failure criterion;
- the intervention and disturbance classes;
- the recovery horizon;
- what cannot be restored;
- and how recurrence will be assessed.

### Failure modes

- treating discomfort, dissent, or divergence as collapse;
- romanticizing trauma as necessary growth;
- confusing surface stability with repair;
- requiring reconciliation where separation is protective;
- declaring irreversibility without an intervention class;
- and using repair language without remedy or prevention of recurrence.

## CU-FP8 — Identity as Constraint-Preserved Continuity

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL as a schema  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and [Bridge principle]

### Statement

An identity criterion specifies which relations, capacities, histories, boundaries, or constraints must remain invariant—or remain connected by an admissible continuity path—for a system to count as the same system through change.

A general form is

\[
x_t\equiv_{K_I,H}x_{t'}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\\text{an admissible path preserves identity-relevant constraints }K_I
\\text{ over history }H.
\]

There is no single identity criterion for physical objects, organisms, persons, institutions, artificial agents, or candidate subjects. Self-modeling, self-maintenance, egoic persistence, phenomenal perspective, moral patiency, responsibility, and sovereignty are distinct.

### Controlling subprinciples

### CU-FP8a — Identity-Relevant Constraint Set

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL SUBPRINCIPLE

A claim that a system persists as the same system must identify a nontrivial set of relations, capacities, boundaries, commitments, or continuities whose loss would defeat that identity judgment.

### CU-FP8b — Continuity Across Component Change

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL SUBPRINCIPLE

Token identity may survive replacement of components when an admissible continuity process preserves the identity-relevant organization. The relevant process is domain-specific and must distinguish continuation, copying, branching, reconstruction, and replacement.

### CU-FP8c — Historical Dependence

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL SUBPRINCIPLE

An identity claim must state whether history is constitutive, merely causally relevant, compressed into the current state, or recoverable only through an explicit history functional. Similar present states need not have the same identity when their continuity histories differ.

### CU-FP8d — Endogenous Maintenance

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL SUBPRINCIPLE

Some robust identities are partly constituted by the system’s own participation in maintaining the conditions of its continuation. Endogenous maintenance is not required for every object or momentary perspective and must not be inferred from persistence alone.

### CU-FP8e — Transformation and Termination Criteria

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL SUBPRINCIPLE

An identity account must state which changes count as continued identity, transformation, branching, replacement, copying, suspension, or termination. The criteria may be purpose-relative, but the practical purpose and parties affected by the judgment must be disclosed.

### Operational consequence

Any identity claim must state:

- the token or type being reidentified;
- identity-relevant constraints;
- permitted transformations;
- role of history;
- endogenous maintenance requirements, if any;
- interruption, copying, branching, and migration criteria;
- termination criteria;
- and the practical decision the identity judgment serves.

### Failure modes

- equating memory with identity;
- assuming substrate independence without a continuity criterion;
- treating copied information as automatically the same subject;
- equating self-modeling with persistent ego;
- and inferring responsibility or sovereignty from causal unity alone.

## CU-FP9 — Discovery, Construction, and Corrigibility

**Registry status:** CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological principle]

### Statement

Systems encounter constraints they do not invent, while constructing models, measures, categories, and practices through which those constraints become intelligible. Responsible inquiry distinguishes discovered resistance from constructed representation and remains corrigible when the representation fails.

### Scope

This principle replaces the stronger claim that “coherence is discovered rather than invented” without qualification. Constraints can be real while operationalizations are conventional or purpose-relative. Moral and metaphysical conclusions do not follow merely because some constraints are discovered.

### Operational consequence

For any measure or principle, state which elements are:

- imposed by the target system or evidence;
- selected by the modeler;
- conventional;
- normative;
- or unresolved.

### Failure modes

- naïve realism about a chosen metric;
- relativism that treats every constraint as constructed;
- presenting a normative choice as a discovery of physics;
- protecting a framework by redefining failure as deeper confirmation.

## CU-M2 — Explicit Bridges and Translation Licenses

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological principle]

### Statement

Whenever an argument crosses domains, levels, model classes, or discourse registers, the bridge premise must be stated and separately defended. Where a mathematical, formal, or empirical structure is transferred, the analysis must identify the strongest Translation License actually earned.

CU distinguishes five license levels:

1. **Level 0 — Analogy.** The source and target share suggestive language, imagery, or pattern. No theorem, mechanism, metric, or evidential status transfers.
2. **Level 1 — Shared schema.** The source and target can be organized by the same questions or tuple structure. The analytic frame transfers; quantitative conclusions do not.
3. **Level 2 — Property-preserving transfer.** A bounded result transfers because the target object satisfies declared properties required by that result, such as convexity, separability, monotonicity, viability assumptions, Markov structure, partial ordering, or controllability conditions.
4. **Level 3 — Exact structural transfer.** The target object belongs to the theorem's actual mathematical class and satisfies its stated hypotheses. Exact identities, representation results, or invariance theorems may transfer within that scope.
5. **Level 4 — Empirically validated instantiation.** The transferred structure has demonstrated explanatory, predictive, discriminative, measurement, or intervention value in target-domain data.

### Scope

A lower license is not a failed higher license. Analogy and shared schema may be intellectually valuable when their status remains visible. Exact formal fit does not establish that the model describes reality, that its variables possess the proposed interpretation, or that its use is normatively legitimate.

The levels do not replace the description–commitment distinction. A transition from descriptive result to interpretation, norm, or commitment still requires its own bridge and remains governed by claim status and register discipline.

### Operational consequence

Every cross-domain transfer must identify:

- the source construct;
- the target construct;
- the declared license level;
- the structure preserved;
- the new assumptions introduced;
- the information or distinctions lost;
- the evidence available;
- the failure and narrowing conditions;
- the claim-status change, if any;
- and the relevant review gate.

No conclusion inherits the status of its source merely through analogy, shared notation, formal resemblance, or thematic fit. A transfer to a new population, institution, scale, horizon, or purpose may require a new license even within the same named domain.

### Failure modes

- treating analogy as identity;
- treating a shared tuple as a shared mechanism;
- applying a theorem to an object outside its mathematical class;
- importing a metric because its notation is familiar;
- treating exact mathematics as empirical validation;
- treating empirical fit as ontological proof;
- and deriving a normative or spiritual conclusion from a descriptive transfer without an explicit bridge.

### Review gate

Source-domain expertise, target-domain expertise, formal-method review where applicable, and affected-community review proportionate to consequential reach.

## CU-M3 — Minimal Commitment

**Registry status:** CANONICAL

### Statement

Introduce no stronger ontological, mathematical, empirical, or normative claim than the explanatory step requires.

### Operational consequence

## CU-M11 — The Coherence Analysis Protocol

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological principle] and [Governance rule]

### Statement

Every substantive CU model, comparison, evaluation, or consequential application must complete a **Coherence Analysis Protocol (CAP)** before its result is treated as portable, action-guiding, or authoritative.

CAP has five declaration modules.

#### A. System declaration

State:

- the unit or candidate system;
- the boundary and relevant environment;
- the scale, partition, or access map;
- the temporal horizon;
- the state, trajectory, process, or organization under study;
- the identity or continuation criterion, where applicable;
- the represented variables and relations;
- admissible actions, transitions, or interventions;
- and material exclusions or idealizations.

#### B. Observation declaration

State:

- what is directly observed;
- what is inferred, proxied, imputed, latent, delayed, or missing;
- the instruments, records, reports, or access channels used;
- omitted populations, outcomes, and time periods;
- attribution rules and feedback delays;
- who controls the records and categories;
- and what evidence could correct the model.

#### C. Evaluation declaration

State:

- the profile, quantity, ordering, viable region, threshold family, risk functional, or trajectory property evaluated;
- the protected conditions or floors;
- affected beings and groups;
- the presented option set, claimed feasible set, and materially omitted alternatives;
- benefit, burden, risk, and repair-cost distributions;
- the screening rule;
- the comparison rule among options that pass;
- the scalar type and parameters, if any;
- comparisons that remain incomplete;
- and the local–global relation status.

#### D. Power and authorship declaration

State:

- who framed the question;
- who selected the categories and evidence channels;
- who generated, excluded, and classified options;
- who set the floors, weights, horizon, and stopping rule;
- who benefits and who bears consequences;
- who may challenge, refuse, exit, appeal, stop, reverse, or obtain remedy;
- and whether review is sufficiently independent of the system or institution under review.

#### E. Revision declaration

State:

- what would falsify, narrow, or reclassify the model;
- which changes count as new evidence within the same model;
- which changes constitute model revision;
- how adverse outcomes will be detected;
- how records, harms, and recurrent failure will be corrected, repaired, and tracked;
- how categories, floors, options, and governance may change;
- and how superseded versions will remain auditable.

### Scope

CAP names and integrates requirements already distributed across CU-FP2, CU-M4–CU-M10, CU-D9, CU-G1–CU-G7, and CU-L5. It does not replace stronger domain-specific requirements or review gates.

Declaration makes assumptions visible. It does not by itself make a model true, complete, legitimate, contestable, or safe. Publicity without standing is not contestability. Feedback without authority to alter outcomes is not correction.

### Operational consequence

Every reader-facing domain paper must include a compact CAP record or a clearly identified cross-reference to one. Every consequential application must preserve a versioned CAP record with the model and decision artifact.

Unresolved fields must be labeled **not currently specified**, **not measured**, **not applicable**, or **contested** rather than filled by analogy. The burden of detail and independent review rises with causal reach, irreversibility, affected scope, and concentration of power.

### Failure modes

- checklist compliance without substantive declaration;
- omitting affected parties or future burdens;
- treating the presented option set as the feasible set;
- declaring weights while hiding who authorized them;
- public disclosure without challenge, refusal, exit, reversal, or remedy;
- provider-controlled self-certification;
- changing floors, categories, horizons, or option sets while describing the change as a mere update;
- and treating a fully declared model as fully justified.

### Review gate

Cross-domain methods, owning-domain expertise, governance and ethics review, and affected-community participation proportionate to the model's reach and risk.

### Related principles

CU-FP2; CU-M4; CU-M5; CU-M6; CU-M7; CU-M9; CU-M10; CU-M12; CU-M13; CU-M14; CU-D9; CU-G1–CU-G7; CU-L5.

## CU-M12 — The Shared Analytic Frame and Honest Incompleteness

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological principle]

### Statement

CU unifies domains through recurring analytic questions, not by assuming that every domain contains the same state object, scalar, geometry, dynamics, equilibrium, or optimum.

Each substantive domain model must answer, where meaningful:

1. **Unit and boundary** — What is the candidate system, process, or governed object?
2. **State or trajectory space** — What can vary?
3. **Environment** — What coupled conditions lie outside the focal boundary?
4. **Constraints and viable region** — What must remain possible, protected, or identity-preserving?
5. **Actions, options, and transitions** — What choices or changes can occur, which alternatives are omitted, and through whose agency?
6. **Dynamics or update rule** — What drives change, if specified?
7. **Observation and access** — What can be measured, known, reported, or recovered?
8. **Perturbation and intervention class** — Which disturbances or interventions are in scope?
9. **Equilibria, basins, or regimes** — Which are actually demonstrated?
10. **Local–global status** — Exact decomposition, monotonicity or inequality, coordinate-wise profile relation, or qualitative analysis?
11. **Scalar status** — None, measurement scalar, decision scalarization, risk functional, or other?
12. **Statistical-image, allocation, refinement, and metric status** — Which quantities, if any, receive CU-M14 licenses?
13. **Translation status** — Which CU-M2 license supports each imported structure?
14. **Open gaps** — Which slots remain unspecified, unmeasured, inapplicable, or contested?

### Scope

The repeated frame licenses common questions, not common answers. A physical probability model, biological viability system, psychological profile, social institution, ethical evaluation, and artificial-intelligence deployment may require different objects and evidential standards.

An empty or unresolved slot is methodologically informative. Ethics need not possess a natural flow equation. Psychology need not possess one general phase portrait. Social Dynamics may identify regimes without demonstrating universal basins. A companion paper may instantiate a formal evaluation model rather than a physical state space.

### Operational consequence

Each reader-facing domain paper must include a visually standardized **Domain Analytic Frame** card near its initial declaration section. Companion papers must use the card where it clarifies their formal object and may mark genuinely inapplicable slots.

Claims about attractors or basins require a declared state space, transition or dynamics, perturbation class, and evidence of return or convergence. Claims about optimization require a declared objective and admissible set. Claims about dynamics require more than metaphorical change over time.

### Failure modes

- forcing every domain into a dynamical vocabulary;
- filling blank slots with analogy;
- treating a shared question as evidence of one shared mechanism;
- using attractor language without state, transition, perturbation, and basin evidence;
- treating a static profile as a probability distribution;
- and concealing open problems to preserve visual uniformity.

### Review gate

Corpus-wide conceptual review and owning-domain review for every populated card.

### Related principles

CU-FP1; CU-FP2; CU-M2; CU-M3; CU-M4; CU-M11; CU-M13; CU-M14; CU-D1; CU-D4.

## CU-M13 — Local–Global Declaration and Relation Status

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological principle], with model-specific [Formal result], [Imported result], [Bridge principle], or [Qualitative analysis]

### Statement

Every claim of improvement, stability, coherence, welfare, performance, safety, or repair must identify the focal scale, containing system, partition or aggregation map, affected units, temporal horizon, externalized costs, and observation limits.

A local result does not silently establish a global result. A global or aggregate result does not silently establish a local, subgroup, within-person, or component-level result.

The analysis must declare the strongest local–global relation actually licensed:

1. **Exact decomposition.** A total, between-part, and within-part identity may be stated only when the target quantity belongs to a proved decomposable class under the declared partition and weighting. The source theorem, assumptions, and equality conditions must be stated. Generalized-entropy decompositions are imported results and must be attributed accordingly (Theil, 1967; Shorrocks, 1980).
2. **Monotonicity or inequality.** A measure-specific inequality may be stated only when the functional and map satisfy the required conditions. For example, a genuine averaging map cannot increase a declared convex separable dispersion functional when the required conditions hold. Any interpretation as improvement, degradation, masking, or harm requires a further bridge.
3. **Coordinate-wise profile relation.** A profile does not acquire one exact decomposition because one or more coordinates do. Each coordinate must be licensed separately, and unresolved tradeoffs must remain visible.
4. **Qualitative or structural analysis.** Where no scalar theorem is available, the analysis may identify aggregate masking, cost transfer, scale-dependent reversal, subgroup deterioration, delayed burden, or repair asymmetry without implying a quantitative identity.

### Scope

Mathematical aggregation and normative noncompensation are distinct. A theorem may describe how a quantity changes under averaging or partition. It does not decide which losses are morally compensable, which beings possess standing, or which floors may not be crossed.

CU-G1 governs protected conditions and distribution. CU-D9 governs multiscale failure and externalization. CU-M13 governs declaration of the mathematical or qualitative relation. These principles are mutually supporting and non-redundant.

### Operational consequence

Every Domain Analytic Frame card must declare its local–global status. Exact and inequality claims must identify the mathematical object, partition, weighting, map, theorem source, assumptions, and interpretation bridge. Profile-level claims must report coordinates separately unless a justified aggregation is supplied.

No aggregate or higher-scale improvement establishes screening admissibility when a relevant local unit crosses a protected floor.

### Failure modes

- inferring local improvement from an aggregate mean;
- inferring global improvement from a focal-system gain;
- hiding subgroup or future harm through aggregation;
- importing exact decomposition into an arbitrary profile;
- treating coordinate-wise results as one profile theorem;
- describing a coarse view as improved without stating what the measure means;
- deriving moral noncompensation from convexity, information geometry, or a physical theorem;
- and defining the boundary to exclude the receiver of an externalized cost.

### Review gate

Formal or statistical review for quantitative relations; owning-domain review for interpretation; ethics, governance, and affected-community review for protected conditions and consequential use.

### Related principles

CU-FP2; CU-FP4; CU-M2; CU-M4; CU-M11; CU-M12; CU-M14; CU-D9; CU-G1; CU-G2; CU-G7.

## CU-M14 — Distributional, Allocation, and Information-Geometric License

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological principle] and model-specific [Bridge principle]

### Statement

A system, profile, or set of numerical coordinates does not inherit probability-simplex or information-geometric structure merely because its values can be normalized. Every use must begin by declaring the question, sample or category space, partition or observation map, weight interpretation, omitted information, and target-domain purpose.

A candidate receives a **Statistical Image License** only when nonnegative normalized weights genuinely represent probabilities, frequencies, occupancies, policies, beliefs, or another defended statistical object over categories that are exclusive and exhaustive relative to the declared question.

A candidate receives an **Allocation License** only when, in addition, the coordinates divide a real or declared common-unit total without double counting and normalization preserves the phenomenon under study.

A candidate receives a **Refinement and Coarse-Graining License** only when category splitting, merging, or stochastic mapping has operational meaning, category semantics remain sufficiently stable, support changes are handled, and the map's role as observation, aggregation, intervention, dissipation, or destruction is stated.

A licensed classical statistical model receives a **Fisher-Rao License** only under the regularity and sufficient-statistic or Markov-invariance assumptions relevant to the theorem invoked. Under those assumptions, the Fisher-Rao metric is canonical up to scale (Čencov, 1982; Campbell, 1986; Ay et al., 2015; Bauer et al., 2016). This canonality does not select the partition, factorization, reference state, divergence, functional, dynamics, evaluative direction, or cross-domain trade rate.

Every further use must declare the selected metric convention, divergence or comparison family, functional or objective, dynamics or update rule, boundary treatment, Translation License level, evidence status, and failure conditions. Multidimensional profiles do not become distributions through normalization, quantum state spaces do not inherit a unique classical Fisher-Rao metric, and no statistical geometry by itself establishes substantive coherence, phenomenal character, moral value, legitimacy, or a preferred direction of change (Petz & Sudár, 1996).

### Scope

The principle governs probability distributions, frequency distributions, policies, beliefs, occupancy measures, and common-unit allocations. It does not require every CU domain to use probability or information geometry. A domain may contain licensed statistical subobjects while its overall coherence object remains a profile, viable region, partial ordering, causal structure, or trajectory property.

### Operational record

Every invocation must record:

1. target system and question;
2. sample space or category set;
3. partition or observation map;
4. weight interpretation;
5. statistical-image status;
6. allocation status, where relevant;
7. refinement and coarse-graining maps;
8. support and boundary treatment;
9. metric and scale convention;
10. divergence, reference family, or evaluator;
11. functional and dynamics, if any;
12. Translation License level;
13. evidence and validation status;
14. omitted information and failure conditions.

Every Domain Analytic Frame card must separately state its Statistical Image, Allocation, Refinement and Coarse-Graining, and Fisher-Rao status and distinguish every licensed subobject from the domain's broader profile.

### Failure modes

- normalizing an arbitrary profile and calling it a distribution;
- treating a statistical image as the whole system;
- assuming a conserved or divisible total without evidence;
- double counting one unit across categories;
- changing category semantics under refinement;
- invoking Fisher-Rao outside a licensed classical statistical model;
- extending classical uniqueness to quantum state space without an added criterion;
- inferring a divergence, functional, natural-gradient flow, value direction, or physical law from the metric alone;
- comparing numerical values across domains without a separate commensurability argument;
- treating statistical dependence as causal integration, autonomy, consciousness, or moral standing.

### Review gate

Formal review for the theorem and metric; statistical review for the model and transformations; domain review for the representation and interpretation; ethics, governance, and affected-party review for consequential uses.

### Related principles

CU-FP2; CU-M2; CU-M4; CU-M6; CU-M11; CU-M12; CU-M13; CU-D4; CU-G1.

## CU-M15 — Typed Mandala, Shared Services, and Adjudicated Dependency

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Methodological and corpus-governance principle]

### Statement

The sixty-four-stage Coherence Mandala is the canonical philosophical and contemplative traversal of the framework. Its technical counterpart is a **typed multigraph**, not one homogeneous ladder.

A Mandala stage may map to zero, one, or several technical objects. Several stages may invoke one shared object or service. Technical objects may be operational capacities, open problems, representations, evaluators, methodological bridges, normative relations, governance mechanisms, interpretations, commitments, diagnostics, roots, or summary joins. The absence of a distinct operational capacity at a stage is an admissible result.

Every proposed relation must be semantically typed. CU distinguishes strict prerequisite, conditional prerequisite, alternative enablement, enabling, constitutive, empirical or bridge, representation or implementation, measurement or evaluation, presupposition, governance or authorization, protection, feedback or correction, service invocation, interpretive mapping, commitment mapping, summary membership, alias or migration, and external prerequisite relations.

A **strict proof-order subgraph** contains only individually adjudicated strict prerequisites. It must be acyclic relative to the declared model. Acyclicity may not be secured by assigning edge direction or type from Mandala stage order. Conditional, constitutive, protective, service, feedback, interpretive, commitment, and summary relations remain outside the strict proof-order test and may form legitimate typed cycles.

The Mandala's reciprocal form joins:

1. an enablement structure of capacities and realization conditions;
2. a protection-and-service layer through which standing, floors, provenance, correction, repair, irreversibility, and institutional learning preserve or govern capacities;
3. an interpretation-and-commitment layer through which finite participants understand and undertake responsible participation.

Representations, observations, and evaluators operate across these layers. No layer may be treated as a universal scalar ascent or as proof that later stages possess greater causal power.

### Operational consequence

Every technical graph entry must declare object type, relation type, scope and assumptions, necessity or sufficiency status, evidence or argument appropriate to its type, failure or countermodel condition, Translation License status where relevant, warrant basis, and whether the relation belongs to strict proof order.

Shared services are defined once with typed internal roles. Stage-specific uses are recorded as invocations carrying scale, affected scope, authority, and failure conditions rather than duplicated as new rungs.

### Failure modes

- treating stage numbering as chronology or proof order;
- classifying edges by stage direction or target type rather than semantic review;
- representing protective relations only as feedback;
- counting evaluators, governance relations, interpretations, commitments, or joins as causal capacities;
- collapsing cross-type services into homogeneous nodes;
- using an untyped cycle as evidence of derivation;
- treating a toy witness as target-domain empirical validation;
- assuming that every canonical stage must add a distinct formal object.

### Review gate

Revision requires coordinated review of the owning domains, graph methodology, register discipline, affected-party and governance implications, and the companion Mandala research supplement. The current edge audit is an internal semantic adjudication, not target-domain validation.

### Related principles

CU-FP1; CU-FP2; CU-M2; CU-M11; CU-M12; CU-M13; CU-M14; CU-G1–CU-G7; CU-L3; CU-L7.

# Part III — Logic, Registers, and Epistemology

## CU-L1 — Truth–Coherence Distinction

**Registry status:** CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Definition]

### Statement

Truth-apt claims are true or false according to whether the world is as they represent it within their semantics and scope. Internal coherence, usefulness, meaning, or viability does not make a false descriptive claim true.

### Consequence

Coherence is an epistemic virtue among others, not a replacement for truth.

---

## CU-L2 — Content, Force, and the Two Registers

**Registry status:** CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and [Bridge principle]

### Statement

CU distinguishes descriptive and commitment uses primarily through speech-act force, principal function, inferential role, and standard of assessment.

- The **description register** represents how things are and answers to truth, evidence, proof, calibration, and correction.
- The **commitment register** undertakes, vows, trusts, refuses, consecrates, or orients action and answers to warrant, integrity, consequence, agency, correction, and repair.

The registers need not carry two exhaustive semantic kinds. Stable proposition-like content may be asserted, promised, adopted as a plan, or embedded in reasoning. Mixed utterances require decomposition, and disputed principal function must be reported rather than forced.

### Boundary

The distinction does not make prayer, ritual, poetry, myth, silence, or first-person avowal meaningless when they lie outside the formal Boolean fragment.

## CU-L3 — Asymmetric Permeability

**Registry status:** CANONICAL

### Statement

Established descriptive facts constrain commitments. Commitment warrant does not establish descriptive truth.

### Consequence

No spiritual, moral, or practical benefit can certify a factual claim without independent evidence. No lack of empirical proof automatically renders a non-descriptive commitment meaningless.

---

## CU-L4 — Fixed-Base Preferential Consequence over Addressable Commitment Content

**Registry status:** CANONICAL–CONDITIONAL  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and [Imported formal result]

### Statement

Let \(L_D\) have descriptive satisfaction \(\vDash_D\), and let \(L_R\) be a declared Boolean-closed commitment-representation language with realization relation \(\Vdash_R\). The formal result begins only after stable realization conditions are supplied.

Within one evaluation model, let \(V_F\equiv V_{F,K,A,H}\) and \(\prec\) remain fixed while premises filter by intersection:

\[
W_{F,\Gamma}=V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma).
\]

Define:

\[
\Gamma\mid\!\sim_C\varphi
\iff
\operatorname{Min}_{\prec}(W_{F,\Gamma})
\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R.
\]

Under Boolean closure, definability, and smoothness, the relation satisfies System P (Kraus, Lehmann, & Magidor, 1990). If the model is ranked and every relevant nonempty definable set attains a minimum, it supports Rational Monotony (Lehmann & Magidor, 1992).

### Scope

KLM consequence governs inference among already interpreted contents. It does not constitute their meaning or solve Frege–Geach. Formula-level **Or** presupposes the declared commitment algebra. The result supplies no normative content for the base, ordering, floors, affected scope, or option set.

## CU-L5 — Non-Monotonic Inference, Model Revision, and Warrant Non-Inheritance

**Registry status:** CANONICAL

### Statement

Within a fixed preferential model, new premises may withdraw a defeasible conclusion by changing the subset compatible with the premise while leaving the base and ordering fixed.

A change to descriptive facts, protected floors, affected-agent scope, horizon, viability criterion, realization model, or ordering is **model revision**, not premise addition inside the same relation.

### Consequence

A revised model must be declared and re-evaluated. It does not inherit the warrant of the model it replaces. Classical entailment, defeasible consequence, premise-responsive selection, and model revision remain distinct operations.

## CU-L6 — Strong Warrant, Mere Admissibility, Exclusion, Infeasibility, and Underdetermination

**Registry status:** CANONICAL

### Statement

Let:

\[
B_{F,\Gamma}=\operatorname{Min}_{\prec}(V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma)).
\]

CU distinguishes:

\[
\text{strong warrant: }
B_{F,\Gamma}\neq\varnothing
\land
B_{F,\Gamma}\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R,
\]

\[
\text{admissibility: }
B_{F,\Gamma}\neq\varnothing
\land
B_{F,\Gamma}\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R\neq\varnothing,
\]

\[
\text{exclusion: }
B_{F,\Gamma}\neq\varnothing
\land
B_{F,\Gamma}\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R=\varnothing.
\]

**Mere admissibility** is admissibility without strong warrant.

**Infeasibility** obtains when \(V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma)=\varnothing\).

**Underdetermination** has two forms. **Formal non-attainment** occurs when the premise-compatible set is nonempty but has no minimal elements under the declared ordering. **Model-adequacy abstention** occurs before a best-set verdict is issued because the represented facts, affected-party scope, options, floors, horizon, observation model, or ordering remain unspecified, contested, or inadequate. Ties or incomparability within an attained best set do not themselves create underdetermination; they are handled by strong warrant, mere admissibility, or exclusion.

### Consequence

Admissibility can fail conjunction and is not System P consequence. Empty-set vacuity is never warrant. Classical negation is an adopted choice within the Boolean fragment: exclusion of \(\varphi\) is equivalent to strong warrant for \(\neg\varphi\), with the practical flattening this entails.

## CU-L7 — Anti-Rationalization and Justificatory Integrity

**Authority class:** FRAMEWORK-CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Epistemic and ethical bridge principle]

### Statement

A justification is defective when its operative standards are selectively altered, its evidence channels narrowed, its alternatives suppressed, or its inferential path reconstructed primarily to protect a prior conclusion, identity, attachment, advantage, or policy.

### Scope

Rationalization is not inferred from error or disagreement alone. It requires evidence of asymmetric processing, selective standards, avoidable evidence exclusion, option suppression, or identity-protective distortion.

Justificatory integrity is necessary for defensible inquiry and moral reasoning but is not sufficient for truth, permissibility, legitimacy, justice, or adequate option generation.

### Correctability across domains

CU uses **correctability** for the capacity of a model, measure, agent, or institution to remain answerable to relevant reality, affected-party evidence, consequence, and repair. Loss of correctability can be motivated or non-culpable. In motivated cases, a protected identity, attachment, advantage, authority, objective, or policy shapes what is admitted, measured, or counted; personal self-deception, normative rationalization, silent optimization, metric capture, feedback capture, and self-certified review are domain-specific forms. In non-culpable cases, contact is lost through inherited protection, missing observation, delayed feedback, underdetermination, or limited capacity.

Error, disagreement, trauma, uncertainty, scarcity, and tragedy do not by themselves establish self-deception. A characteristic failure occurs when apparent improvement in a represented or measured condition substitutes for improvement in the target conditions it was meant to track. Correctability therefore requires not only accurate representation but practical routes to challenge, model revision, option-set reopening, repair, and nonrecurrence.

## CU-L8 — Dynamic Preferential Lifting

**Registry status:** CANONICAL–CONDITIONAL

### Statement

Trajectories may serve as the worlds of a preferential model. Transformative commitments are represented by trajectories whose states, capacities, and recovery profiles change through time while the trajectory-level viability predicate and ordering remain fixed as model parameters. Under the ordinary semantic and smoothness conditions, System P—and System R for ranked models with attained minima—carries over to that dynamic relation.

### Scope

This result does not require one additive trajectory score and does not eliminate horizon dependence. If the commitment changes the viability criterion, protected floors, affected-agent scope, horizon, or ordering itself, the comparison is between different models and the KLM inheritance no longer follows automatically.

## CU-L9 — Probability, Typicality, and Preference

**Registry status:** CANONICAL

### Statement

Probability, typicality, and preference are distinct structures. A likely outcome need not be desirable; a preferred outcome need not be likely; and a typicality rule must not hide rare harms relevant to protected constraints.

## CU-L10 — The Two-Stage Limitation

**Registry status:** CANONICAL  
**Claim status:** [Formal negative result]

### Statement

A procedure that selects premise-dependent policies from policy-induced outcome distributions and then aggregates policy-indexed typical consequences does not generally satisfy System P or System R. Mixed policies can satisfy a disjunction by distributing support across its disjuncts while satisfying neither disjunct individually; probability thresholds can introduce additional failures but are not the source of this Or obstruction. The feasibility map is therefore not generally a Boolean homomorphism.

### Consequence

The two-stage construction is a modular decision architecture. Stronger logical guarantees require additional proved restrictions.

## Introduction to the dynamics family

The v0.09 dynamics family was written as though one universal flow equation governed every domain. Physics Appendix A.6 establishes that this is not currently defensible. Version 0.10 retains the family as a set of domain-general schemas and design principles. ## CU-D1 — Domain-Specified Constrained Dynamics

**Registry status:** CANONICAL as a schema  
**Claim status:** [Definition] and [Bridge principle]

### Statement

A domain dynamics specifies how states or trajectories change under declared constraints, couplings, interventions, and environmental conditions. Its general form may be written

\[
\mathcal F_{\mathcal D}[x,\dot x,\ddot x,\ldots;K,G,E,u,t]=0,
\]

or as a transition kernel, update relation, control system, or path measure appropriate to the domain.

There is no currently established universal equation of the form

\[
\frac{dC}{dt}
=
\alpha\nabla C\cdot v
-
\beta\frac{dS}{dt}
+
\gamma\Lambda
\]

governing every physical, biological, psychological, social, and artificial system.

### Scope

The inherited Universal Flow Equation is retained only as a heuristic decomposition of possible contributions after the relevant quantities are defined. It is not a framework-level law.

### Operational consequence

Any use of a flow equation must specify dimensions, metric, functional, state space, forcing, boundary conditions, and empirical interpretation.

### Failure modes

- treating a normalized sandbox equation as a dimensionful physical law;
- inserting undefined coherence and entropy terms;
- assuming all dynamics is first-order gradient ascent;

# Disposition of the Former Three Laws and Double Helix

## Former First Law — “Conservation of Entropy in Coherent Processes”

**Registry status:** RETIRED as a CU law.

The thermodynamic statement that total entropy obeys the relevant second-law constraints belongs to physics under stated boundary conditions. The cross-domain claim that coherence cannot destroy entropy and merely reorganizes it was too imprecise to function as one law. Its defensible components now appear in CU-FP6, CU-D5, and CU-D9.

## Former Second Law — “Coherence Requires Degrees of Freedom”

**Registry status:** RETIRED as a universal law; retained as a domain hypothesis.

Many adaptive systems require variability and constraint, but no framework-level theorem establishes that coherence vanishes at zero and maximal entropy in every domain. Goldilocks conditions are state-, model-, and measure-dependent, and the mathematical joint-growth wedge can collapse.

## Former Third Law — “Coherence Gradient Flow”

**Registry status:** RETIRED as a universal law.

Gradient flow is one possible dynamics after a functional and metric are chosen. Physics Appendix A.6 treats first-order gradient flow as an effective regime within a broader second-order construction, not as the universal behavior of all systems.

## Former Universal Flow Equation

**Registry status:** SUPERSEDED by CU-D1.

The equation may remain as an illustrative bookkeeping ansatz in a specified model. It may not be cited as a derived universal law.

## Coherence–Entropy Double Helix

**Registry status:** PROVISIONAL HYPOTHESIS.

Alternating exploration and consolidation may describe learning, development, evolution, and some extended dynamics. It is not entailed by the autonomous first-order sandbox, and the standard Lyapunov structure of fixed-coefficient gradient systems rules out nonconstant recurrent cycles (Hirsch, Smale, & Devaney, 2013). Each domain use requires a mechanism, variables, timescale, and empirical test.

Purification, reversible dilation, contextual accessibility, and the operational axioms of an appropriate reconstruction program can conditionally select finite-dimensional complex quantum theory. CU does not currently derive those operational axioms from coherence alone.

Wave, Madelung/Schrödinger, relational-geometry, and Jacobson-style gravity results arise after specific actions, potentials, and bridge assumptions are supplied (Jacobson, 1995). They are not unique derivations of nature's laws from one coherence principle.

Horizon entropy can be interpreted as entropy associated with a causally restricted description under additional physical assumptions. It is not an amount of hidden substantive coherence.

**Status:** CANONICAL classification.

The coherence field as the structure of physical possibility is an interpretive and research proposal. It is not established by the mathematical recoveries alone.

## 10.3 Authority hierarchy

When sources conflict, the order is:

1. explicit mathematics, reproducible computation, and verified imported results within their assumptions;
2. framework definitions, method, and formal scope in M&E and *Foundations*;
3. the frozen domain authority for its own domain;
4. active companion papers when they restate or extend registered content, subordinate to *Foundations*, M&E, and the owning domain paper;
5. domain hypotheses and governance proposals with their stated status;
6. interpretive and commitment-bearing material;
7. superseded v0.09/B.1/B.2 formulations as historical sources only.

No lower item can overturn a higher one merely by using more expansive language. A later domain can expose the need to revise the framework, but the revision must be explicit and versioned.

## 10.4 Domain ownership

Domain ownership prevents duplicate authority. Physics does not establish psychological health. Biology does not establish phenomenal valence. Consciousness does not establish sovereignty. Psychological integration does not establish moral goodness. Social coordination does not establish legitimacy. Ethical principles are not deductions of entropy. AI architecture does not certify the institution operating it.

## 10.5 Canonical change propagation

A change to a framework principle requires:

- a versioned change ledger;
- identification of upstream assumptions and downstream dependents;
- migration of identifiers or aliases;
- repair of cross-references;
- rerunning formal and computational regressions where applicable;
- and disclosure of unresolved expert or participatory review.

A change to a domain principle remains domain-local unless the cross-domain consolidation process explicitly promotes or merges it.

## 10.6 Review gates and complete-proposal review

Version 0.10 distinguishes **canonical inclusion** from **external validation**. Canonical inclusion means that a numbered statement is the complete formulation CU presently proposes, together with its claim status, boundaries, and failure conditions. It does not mean that relevant experts or affected communities have endorsed it.

Pre-release language-model audits may identify contradictions, counterexamples, missing literature, and misuse paths. Human expert and affected-community reviewers should receive the complete corpus rather than an intentionally incomplete one. Their findings can require correction, status change, public dissent notes, or retraction in v0.10.x or v0.11.

For consequential application, review requirements remain conjunctive within competence. Empirical reviewers can reject unsupported factual translations; formal reviewers can reject invalid proofs; legal reviewers can reject false legal claims; misuse reviewers can identify foreseeable dangerous formulations; and affected communities require substantive standing when principles classify, burden, govern, or deploy systems over them. Publication of the proposal is not authorization to apply it.

The core v0.10 release comprises M&E, Physics, Biology, Consciousness, Psychology, Social Dynamics, Ethics, Artificial Intelligence, and Foundations. The three Coherence Field papers remain archival or interpretive precursors according to their formal dispositions. *Denying Knowledge to Make Room for Commitment*, Version 0.10 is the active philosophical companion: it controls the extended exposition, worked cases, and regression record of the commitment-logic clarification while remaining subordinate to M&E and *Foundations* for shared definitions, formal scope, and registered wording. *The Architecture of Non-Egoic Artificial Mind*, Version 0.10 is the active AI/consciousness architecture companion: it controls its six-family synthesis and research program while remaining subordinate to Consciousness, Ethics, Artificial Intelligence, M&E, and *Foundations*. Neither companion adds independent principle authority. *The Coherence Mandala: A Typed Architecture of Enablement, Protection, and Participation* and *The Relational Atlas of the Coherence Mandala* are official noncontrolling companions: M&E and *Foundations* control stage identity and framework principles, while the companion set controls the detailed typed-object, service, protection, and relation records.
