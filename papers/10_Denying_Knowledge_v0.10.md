# Abstract

Religious and metaphysical discourse has often been forced into a choice between two unsatisfactory positions. Either its central utterances are treated as descriptions of hidden states of affairs and judged by standards they frequently cannot meet, or they are treated as expressions, practices, or forms of life that no longer answer to truth—and then appear to answer to nothing at all. The first route turns faith into speculative science. The second produces the insulation objection: if commitment is removed from descriptive evaluation without receiving another standard, nothing internal to the practice can count against it.

This paper distinguishes a **description register** from a **commitment register** primarily by speech-act function and standard of assessment, not by positing two exhaustive semantic species. The same stable content may be asserted, hoped, vowed, trusted, refused, ritualized, or adopted as a plan. Descriptive uses remain answerable to truth and evidence. Commitment uses are assessed by warrant, integrity, protected viability, affected-party standing, consequence, correction, and repair. The registers are asymmetrically permeable: established facts constrain commitment, while commitment does not manufacture fact.

For the representable fragment, the paper assumes a Boolean-closed language whose commitment contents possess stable **realization conditions**. It does not derive those conditions and does not offer a general solution to the Frege–Geach problem. Preferential consequence operates at a later level: given stable contents, it asks what follows defeasibly across the best viable realizations of a fixed evaluation model. Under standard smoothness and definability conditions, the plain fixed-base preferential consequence relation has the KLM System P structure. Strong warrant adds feasibility: it coincides with that relation on feasible premises and is reported separately from infeasibility. Ranked models support Rational Monotony when relevant minima are attained.

The resulting practical taxonomy separates **strong warrant**, **mere admissibility**, **exclusion**, **infeasibility**, and **underdetermination**. A separate practical-expansion review asks whether a commitment may responsibly be adopted once facts, protected floors, affected agents, option-set adequacy, uncertainty, horizon, reversibility, independent challenge, and revision conditions are included. The paper’s distinctive proposal is therefore not a new general semantics of commitment. It is the combination of stable commitment contents with fixed-base nonmonotonic consequence, yielding distinctions between what every best realization supports and what at least one supports.

The paper applies this architecture to the insulation objection, embedded inference, disagreement, transformative commitment, deferred inquiry, political identity, and a schematic case of two widows who share the same doctrine but inhabit it differently. It also treats the register discipline reflexively: adopting it is itself a defeasible commitment grounded in a broader commitment to truth. Description remains corrigible by evidence; commitment remains corrigible by fact, consequence, affected-party testimony, and the trajectories it generates.

The result is neither fideism nor a general non-cognitivism. It is an account of how responsible commitment remains possible after counterfeit knowledge has been denied.

**Keywords:** commitment; religious language; non-monotonic logic; preferential semantics; System P; speech acts; Wittgenstein; Frege–Geach; self-deception; spiritual practice; warrant.

# 1. Introduction: Two Widows

Consider a schematic case.

Two women lose a husband within a month of each other. Both belong to the same congregation, and both hold the same commitments: the dead are with God, death is not the end, grief is not despair.

The first concludes that to grieve would be to doubt, and does not grieve. She speaks of her husband’s homegoing with composure. Within a year she cannot say his name aloud. She has stopped visiting the grave, avoids the couple they used to see on Fridays, and has become brittle with anyone else in the congregation who is mourning. Asked how she is, she says she is at peace, and she is not lying.

The second concludes that grief is what love does when it loses its object, and that hope does not cancel loss. She grieves badly for two years. She keeps saying his name. At the end of it she is someone who can sit with other people in their losses, and her faith has not diminished.

The example stipulates rather than clinically diagnoses the relevant pattern. Quiet, delayed, private, culturally unfamiliar, or nonexpressive grief is not by itself evidence of suppression. Behavioral signs alone never establish the inner organization of a bereaved person. On the stipulated facts, however, one form of commitment has become insulated from the loss it claims to interpret, while the other has remained answerable to it.

The two women share a theological picture. The difference lies in how that picture is inhabited. Nothing in the case settles whether the dead are with God. Sincerity does not settle the difference either. What matters is whether the commitment remains in truthful contact with experience, agency, relationship, and revision.

This paper asks what standard can locate that difference.

## 1.1 The insulation problem

A familiar response to evidential criticism of religion holds that religious utterances do not always function like scientific hypotheses. They may orient a form of life. They may express trust, dependence, repentance, gratitude, hope, identity, promise, and practical allegiance. Demanding the evidential standard appropriate to ordinary empirical description may therefore misidentify what the utterance is doing.

This response explains much. It explains why religious language persists despite evidential pressure that would defeat an empirical hypothesis. It explains why a creed can remain practically central even when adherents disagree about literal interpretation. It explains why ritual, prayer, lament, and blessing cannot be reduced to reports about invisible objects.

But the response creates a debt. If religious commitment is removed from descriptive standards and no other standard is supplied, nothing can count against it. A disciplined commitment and a destructive one become equally protected. A political or spiritual community can reinterpret every failure as confirmation. The protection of faith becomes protection from criticism.

That is the insulation objection.

Its force is not that every religious sentence must be a failed empirical claim. Its force is that meaningful commitment must remain vulnerable to corruption and revision even when its principal work is not descriptive.

## 1.2 The historical challenge

The problem inherits the shape of several older debates. Ayer's verificationist challenge supplied one influential form of the demand that cognitively meaningful assertion answer to publicly assessable conditions (Ayer, 1936).

Hume’s discussion of miracle testimony asks how extraordinary religious claims should be weighed against established experience (Hume, 1748/2000, §10). Clifford turns evidential responsibility into a moral demand: belief formed without adequate evidence can itself be culpable (Clifford, 1877). Wisdom’s invisible-gardener parable, later used in the Flew–Hare–Mitchell symposium, sharpens the challenge of endlessly qualified religious assertion (Wisdom, 1945; Flew, 1955; Hare, 1955; Mitchell, 1955). Geach’s challenge to non-cognitivist and ascriptivist accounts asks how a content can retain its identity when embedded rather than asserted (Geach, 1960).

One family of replies meets these challenges inside the description register. Natural theology, Alston’s doxastic-practice account, and Plantinga’s proper-function account defend religious belief as truth-apt and potentially warranted (Alston, 1991; Plantinga, 2000). Nothing in this paper refutes that project. If a religious proposition purports to describe reality, it remains answerable to the evidence and arguments appropriate to that claim.

A second family emphasizes that religious discourse also performs practical and expressive work. Kant limits theoretical knowledge to make room for practical faith. Hare’s *blik*, Wittgensteinian philosophy of religion, Phillips’s grammar of religious life, and expressivist approaches treat some utterances as orienting practice rather than merely picturing hidden fact.

The second family sees something important and leaves something unresolved. Protecting a practice from a category mistake does not yet explain what distinguishes a truthful, revisable commitment from an insane, coercive, self-deceptive, or destructive one.

This paper takes that unresolved problem as its point of departure.

## 1.3 The proposal

Human discourse has at least two principal functions relevant here.

The **description register** contains uses whose operative role is to represent how things are. Such uses are answerable to evidence, truth, valid inference, calibration, explanatory comparison, and proof relative to explicit assumptions.

The **commitment register** contains uses whose operative role is to orient identity, trust, attention, practice, relationship, or action. Their central question is not exhausted by whether the embedded content describes a hidden object. It is whether the commitment can be responsibly undertaken and inhabited.

The distinction is therefore primarily one of speech-act force, principal function, and assessment standard. It does not require the claim that every commitment content belongs to a different semantic species. “God is with me” may function as a literal metaphysical assertion, a declaration of trust, a ritual avowal, a poetic condensation of dependence, or a mixed utterance. The proper analysis decomposes those functions rather than assigning whole traditions to one category.

The registers are distinct but not sealed:

> **Established descriptive facts constrain commitments. Commitments do not establish descriptive facts.**

This asymmetry is the first defense against insulation. A commitment that requires denial of established fact is unwarranted in that respect. A commitment that helps someone live does not thereby prove its cosmology.

The second defense is a formal and practical architecture of warrant. It must distinguish what follows throughout every best realization from what remains available in only some; what is excluded; what is infeasible; what the model cannot yet decide; and what may responsibly be adopted only under wider social and ethical constraints.

## 1.4 The distinctive contribution

The paper makes six claims.

First, the distinction between description and commitment is best understood primarily through principal function and assessment standard. Stable proposition-like content can appear in both registers while being used and evaluated differently.

Second, commitment-register discourse requires an internal standard. Sincerity, social acceptance, identity, and psychological usefulness are insufficient.

Third, the formal apparatus assumes stable realization conditions for a Boolean-representable fragment. It does not derive a general semantics for religious, ritual, poetic, or contemplative language, and the KLM layer is orthogonal to that compositional task.

Fourth, given stable commitment contents, fixed-base preferential semantics supplies a nonmonotonic consequence relation. It distinguishes what every best viable realization supports from what at least one supports.

Fifth, responsible adoption requires more than formal consequence. It depends on factual compatibility, protected floors, affected-party standing, option-set integrity, independent challenge, uncertainty, horizon, reversibility, and revision conditions.

Sixth, adopting the two-register discipline is itself a commitment: a revisable undertaking to let truth constrain belief and to assess commitments by what they make of persons, relationships, and institutions.

The paper’s distinctive proposal is therefore narrower than a general semantics of commitment and stronger for being so:

> **Representable commitment contents can retain stable content while being governed by defeasible rather than classical practical consequence, yielding distinct verdicts of strong warrant, mere admissibility, exclusion, infeasibility, and underdetermination.**

The aim is not to settle every question in philosophy of religion. It is to show how commitment can remain disciplined after it has ceased to impersonate knowledge.

# 2. Content, Force, and Two Registers

## 2.1 Content is not identical with speech-act force

A sentence can contribute stable content while being used in different ways. “I will care for my mother” can be a prediction, a promise, a vow, a reassurance, or a report of intention. The content relevant to inference may remain stable while the act performed and the standards governing it change.

This paper therefore does not define the two registers by a sharp partition between proposition-like and non-proposition-like meanings. It distinguishes principal functions:

| Principal use | Content and force | Primary assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive assertion | A content is presented as representing how things are | Evidence, truth, inference |
| Commitment act | A content is adopted, promised, trusted, refused, consecrated, or used to orient action | Warrant, integrity, trajectory, agency, correction |
| Symbolic, ritual, poetic, or contemplative performance | Meaning may depend heavily on practice, embodiment, or first-person use | Practice-specific interpretation; not automatically covered by the formal model |
| Semantically empty or manipulatively insulated use | No stable descriptive, inferential, practical, or corrective role can be identified | Critique, clarification, or rejection |

The rows are not natural kinds. One utterance may perform several functions at once.

## 2.2 The description register

An utterance functions descriptively when it purports to represent an independently assessable state of affairs. Examples include:

- a historical claim about an event;
- a causal claim about illness or healing;
- a cosmological claim about the age of the Earth;
- a metaphysical claim used literally;
- a prediction about what a practice will do;
- a psychological claim about grief.

Such claims do not leave the description register because they concern religion, morality, consciousness, or ultimate reality. If they describe, they answer to descriptive standards.

## 2.3 The commitment register

An utterance functions committally when its principal role is to organize how an agent or community proceeds. It may vow, bless, mourn, promise, trust, refuse, consecrate, orient, or receive an event within a practical horizon.

Commitment-register questions include:

- What does this commitment train the agent to notice or exclude?
- Does it preserve contact with established fact?
- Does it integrate experience or require insulation?
- Does it protect or disable agency?
- Whose costs does it externalize?
- What happens when it encounters grief, conflict, uncertainty, or power?
- Can it be revised without destroying the person or community that holds it?

A commitment act can be unwarranted even when its content is true, because descriptive truth does not settle whether that content should be adopted as a vow, plan, identity, or governing orientation.

## 2.4 Mixed discourse

The most important religious utterances are often mixed. Consider:

> Human beings are expressions of one sacred whole; therefore each person should be treated with reverence.

The sentence may contain:

1. a metaphysical assertion or interpretation;
2. a commitment to sacred regard;
3. a normative conclusion.

The moral attractiveness of the conclusion does not prove the metaphysical assertion. The commitment need not collapse merely because the metaphysical picture remains uncertain. The picture can be held as an interpretation if its status is explicit and it remains compatible with fact.

Decomposition allows a tradition to preserve meaning without protecting every inherited factual claim from correction.

## 2.5 How principal function is attributed

Register classification is itself interpretive and can be disputed. No single grammatical form decides it. Relevant evidence includes:

- what the speaker says they are doing;
- what kinds of correction they accept;
- how the utterance behaves in inference;
- what actions, permissions, or prohibitions it generates;
- how the community treats dissent or failed expectation;
- whether the content is asserted across contexts or used mainly in ritual;
- and what institutional consequences depend on taking it literally.

Speaker intention matters but is not conclusive. Institutions can use symbolic language to perform covert descriptive or coercive work. Conversely, an outside observer can misclassify prayer or lament by ignoring first-person practice. When the principal function remains genuinely disputed, the framework should report that uncertainty rather than force a register assignment.

## 2.6 Asymmetric permeability

The relation between registers is one-way with respect to evidential support.

A commitment is not responsibly available when its operative use requires systematic denial of established descriptive constraints.

But the converse does not hold. A commitment may be beautiful, healing, coherent, or morally fruitful without proving its descriptive picture.

Commitments may still influence inquiry by directing attention, generating hypotheses, sustaining costly investigation, and shaping which questions are asked. Inquiry is not value-free. Yet motivation and salience are not evidence, and a commitment’s practical warrant does not establish the truth of the hypotheses it inspires.

This asymmetry blocks two opposed errors:

- **fideistic insulation:** commitment is treated as beyond factual constraint;
- **reductive elimination:** every form of meaning is forced into empirical description.

The discipline is to refuse both.

## 2.7 The two-register discipline is itself a commitment

The distinction is not a view from nowhere. Its descriptive component is a philosophical proposal about discourse, inference, and practice. Choosing to organize inquiry and spiritual life by it is itself a defeasible commitment.

That commitment is grounded in a broader undertaking:

> **Make reality more important than the identity built around one’s interpretation of it.**

The discipline asks us not to let spiritual desire, fear, belonging, or moral aspiration determine what is accepted as fact. It also asks us not to reduce grief, faith, love, purpose, or sacred orientation to failed science.

Both registers remain revisable. Descriptions change with evidence and argument. Commitments change when they depend on false premises, require self-deception, degrade reality contact, foreclose grief or inquiry, protect domination, or generate trajectories incompatible with truth, agency, compassion, justice, and repair.

# 3. The Formal Architecture

## 3.1 What the formal layer assumes

The formal layer does not create the meanings on which it operates. It begins only after a relevant fragment of commitment discourse has been decomposed into stable, representable contents.

Let:

- \(L_D\) be a descriptive language with ordinary truth-satisfaction \(w\vDash_D\delta\);
- \(L_R\) be a declared commitment-representation language;
- \(w\Vdash_R\varphi\) mean that world or trajectory \(w\) **realizes** or **supports** commitment content \(\varphi\).

For the fragment to which the full KLM result is applied, \(L_R\) is Boolean-closed and the realization extensions satisfy:

\[
\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket_R
=
W\setminus\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R,
\]

\[
\llbracket\varphi\land\psi\rrbracket_R
=
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R
\cap
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_R,
\]

\[
\llbracket\varphi\lor\psi\rrbracket_R
=
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R
\cup
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_R.
\]

This is an adopted representation of stable commitment content. It is extension-like and proposition-like enough for embedded inference. A critic may therefore regard it as relabeling propositions rather than supplying a new semantics. The paper accepts that limitation. Its distinctive claim concerns the consequence relation and verdict taxonomy built over these contents, not the original constitution of their meaning.

For ritual, prayer, poetry, myth, first-person avowal, and other contents lacking an adequate Boolean representation, the full System P claim is not made.

## 3.2 Evaluation models

Let:

\[
M=(W,V_F,\prec,\Vdash_R)
\]

be a preferential model for a declared evaluation context.

- \(W\) is a set of worlds, states, or trajectories.
- \(F\) is the established descriptive background.
- \(K\) is the set of protected or hard constraints.
- \(A\) is the affected-agent scope.
- \(H\) is the temporal horizon.
- \(V_F\equiv V_{F,K,A,H}\subseteq W\) is the **fixed viability base** for that model.
- \(\prec\) is a declared preference relation fixed within the model.
- \(\Vdash_R\) supplies the stable realization conditions for the representable commitment fragment.

For a finite commitment theory \(\Gamma\subseteq L_R\), define:

\[
\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma)
=
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket_R,
\]

with \(\operatorname{Real}_R(\varnothing)=W\), and:

\[
W_{F,\Gamma}
=
V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma).
\]

Premises filter a fixed base by intersection. They do not silently change protected floors, affected-agent scope, horizon, or preference order. If any of those changes, the analyst has moved to a different model. That may be legitimate, but it is model revision rather than ordinary premise addition.

For \(X\subseteq W\), let:

\[
\operatorname{Min}_{\prec}(X)
=
\{w\in X:\nexists v\in X\text{ with }v\prec w\}.
\]

Write:

\[
B(F,\Gamma)
=
\operatorname{Min}_{\prec}(W_{F,\Gamma}).
\]

The best set may contain one realization, many tied or incomparable realizations, or—on infinite or non-well-founded structures—none.

The standardized Domain Analytic Frame above is the compact CAP record for this companion paper. It locates the KLM result at exact structural transfer only inside the declared formal fragment and prevents that formal status from silently transferring to model adequacy or practical permission.

## 3.3 Strong warrant and fixed-base preferential consequence

Define strong warrant by:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\forall}\varphi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B(F,\Gamma)\neq\varnothing
\;\land\;
B(F,\Gamma)\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R.
\]

Every best viable realization supports \(\varphi\), and at least one best realization exists. This is the practical verdict of **strong warrant**.

Define the plain fixed-base preferential consequence relation separately:

\[
\Gamma\mid\!\sim_C\varphi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Min}_{\prec}(V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma))
\subseteq
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R.
\]

It is not an object-language conditional. It presupposes the realization semantics of \(L_R\); it does not explain that semantics.

Under the standard definability and smoothness assumptions, this plain relation satisfies the KLM System P rules (Kraus, Lehmann, & Magidor, 1990). Ranked extensions support Rational Monotony when the relevant minima are attained (Lehmann & Magidor, 1992). Strong warrant coincides with the plain relation on feasible premises and fails Reflexivity on infeasible ones, which is why infeasibility is reported as a separate verdict.

The **Or** rule is available only because the representable fragment is Boolean-closed and:

\[
\llbracket\alpha\lor\beta\rrbracket_R
=
\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_R
\cup
\llbracket\beta\rrbracket_R.
\]

For unformalized sets of practices or commitments without a defined disjunction, no full System P claim is made.

If the preference is ranked and every relevant nonempty definable set attains a minimum rank, Rational Monotony is also supported. Total ordering by itself is not enough when minimum attainment can fail.

These results establish the shape of the inference. They do not supply the normative or spiritual content of the viability base and ordering.

## 3.4 Admissibility and mere admissibility

Define inclusive admissibility:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\exists}\varphi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B(F,\Gamma)\neq\varnothing
\;\land\;
B(F,\Gamma)\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R\neq\varnothing.
\]

At least one best viable realization supports \(\varphi\).

A commitment is **merely admissible** when it is admissible but not strongly warranted:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\exists}\varphi
\quad\land\quad
F,\Gamma\not\models_C^{\forall}\varphi.
\]

The distinction matters only where the best set contains more than one relevant realization. In the three-world finite regression, all 294 merely-admissible verdicts occur in the explicitly isolated non-singleton-best-set stratum. The frequencies protect the implementation; they are not evidence that one practical verdict is common in lived commitment.

Admissibility is weaker than consequence. Two commitments can each be admissible while their conjunction is not.

Let:

\[
B(F,\Gamma)=\{w_1,w_2\},
\]

with:

\[
w_1\Vdash_R\varphi\land\neg\psi,
\qquad
w_2\Vdash_R\neg\varphi\land\psi.
\]

Then \(\varphi\) and \(\psi\) are each admissible, but \(\varphi\land\psi\) is not. Admissibility therefore fails **And** and is not itself a System P consequence relation.

## 3.5 Exclusion and classical negation

Define:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\bot}\varphi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B(F,\Gamma)\neq\varnothing
\;\land\;
B(F,\Gamma)\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R=\varnothing.
\]

No best viable realization supports \(\varphi\). The commitment is **excluded** relative to the declared model.

Under the adopted classical complement clause for \(\neg\varphi\), exclusion is equivalent to strong warrant for the formal negation:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\bot}\varphi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\forall}\neg\varphi.
\]

This is a simplifying choice with a practical consequence. “No best realization supports continuing this practice” may not be identical in ordinary deliberation to “every best realization supports ceasing it.” The difference can involve non-applicability, abstention, suspension, or absence of the question. Capturing those distinctions requires a richer action language or non-classical negation. The present paper does not undertake that extension.

Exclusion does not mean physical impossibility. It means that \(\varphi\) is unsupported throughout the best set of the declared model.

## 3.6 Infeasibility

The commitment theory is **infeasible** within the declared model when:

\[
W_{F,\Gamma}
=
V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma)
=
\varnothing.
\]

Nothing survives the conjunction of descriptive background, protected conditions, and commitment premises. This is different from uncertainty. It says that the commitment set cannot be realized inside the model as declared.

## 3.7 Underdetermination

The case is **underdetermined** when premise-compatible viable realizations remain but the evaluation cannot responsibly return one of the three best-set verdicts. This may occur because:

- a relevant nonempty set has no attained minimum;
- facts remain unsettled;
- affected parties or protected floors are incompletely represented;
- the horizon is unspecified;
- the causal model is inadequate;
- or uncertainty is too great for responsible comparison.

Ties do not by themselves create underdetermination. When a nonempty best set exists, ties are exactly what make mere admissibility distinguishable from strong warrant and exclusion.

In the finite strict-partial-order regression, every nonempty candidate set has at least one minimum. The 4,104 empty-best cases are therefore **infeasible**, not underdetermined. Underdetermination belongs to non-attainment and model-adequacy conditions not represented by that finite suite.

Underdetermination is not permission by default. It is a demand for inquiry, caution, reversible action, model comparison, or honest unresolved disagreement.

## 3.8 The inherited maximum-preservation clause

An earlier formulation asked whether restricting the feasible trajectory set by adding \(\varphi\) preserved or increased the maximum achievable score. But the restricted set is a subset of the original set, so its maximum cannot increase. Equality holds only when at least one old optimum remains available.

Thus the maximum-preservation condition captures:

\[
\operatorname{Argmax}(F,\Gamma)
\cap
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R
\neq\varnothing,
\]

which is existential support by an old optimum—admissibility—not skeptical consequence.

The correction does not destroy the project. It reveals that the original proposal contained two valid questions and treated them as one.

## 3.9 Practical expansion warrant and captured models

Formal consequence does not determine whether an agent may responsibly add a commitment. A commitment can preserve one best trajectory while:

- violating a protected floor;
- imposing concentrated costs on others;
- obtaining local stability through coercion;
- depending on an artificially truncated option set;
- externalizing harm across scales or generations;
- or becoming safe only under an unrealistic model.

Let practical expansion warrant be written schematically as:

\[
\mathcal W_C(\varphi\mid F,\Gamma,K,H,A,M,U).
\]

A commitment is responsibly adoptable only if, at minimum:

1. its operative use is compatible with established fact;
2. at least one viable continuation remains;
3. no inviolable floor is violated;
4. affected parties and distributions are represented;
5. local integration is not purchased by disabling another agent’s agency;
6. externalized costs across scales and horizons are disclosed;
7. the option set has not been controlled so as to predetermine the verdict;
8. the result is open to sufficiently independent challenge;
9. practical correction, appeal, and remedy exist where the commitment acquires institutional power;
10. the result is sufficiently robust under reasonable model uncertainty;
11. the commitment remains revisable;
12. where severe irreversible harm is possible, retained option value or a necessity-and-proportionality exception is supplied.

Public specification is necessary but insufficient. It makes capture visible; it does not make the model contestable. The formal apparatus supplies a verdict structure and no protection against a captured viability base, ordering, option set, or affected-party model. Those protections come from the normative governance architecture developed in *Foundations*, *Social Dynamics*, and *Ethics*.

The verdicts are therefore **model-relative**. When several people genuinely share the same model, they can reproduce the same result. The formalism does not guarantee convergence across rival models, and transparent unresolved disagreement may be the correct endpoint.

Practical expansion warrant is not a single theorem. It is a governance architecture for responsible adoption.

# 4. Addressability and the Boundary of the Formalization

## 4.1 Meaning exceeds formal addressability

A theory that recognizes commitment-register meaning still needs to distinguish three questions:

1. Is the utterance meaningful within a practice?
2. Is its relevant content stable enough for public criticism and embedded inference?
3. Is that content representable in the Boolean realization language used by the formal model?

The questions do not have the same answer in every case. Prayer, ritual, poetry, myth, silence, and contemplative avowal may be meaningful without admitting a complete Boolean decomposition. Such cases are **outside the present formalization**, not thereby meaningless.

## 4.2 Addressable commitment content

A commitment is addressable to the degree that its adoption makes a stable, criticizable difference to what follows. It may:

- require or forbid actions;
- alter which reasons count;
- organize attention;
- change the interpretation of loss or obligation;
- structure relationships;
- create expectations about how the agent should proceed;
- expose the agent to criticism if its own standards are violated;
- and specify conditions of revision or release.

For formal treatment, those differences must be representable through stable realization conditions across the contexts in which the formulas appear.

## 4.3 A graded criterion

A commitment \(\varphi\) has commitment-register content to the degree that adding it to \(\Gamma\) changes:

- the realizable trajectory set;
- the practical priorities or ordering;
- the actions licensed or prohibited;
- the reasons an agent must answer;
- the identity or relationship undertaken;
- or the conditions under which revision is required.

It is **formally addressable** only where enough of that structure can be represented without falsifying the practice.

This criterion is graded. Some utterances have rich public commitment content. Others function mainly as ritual, aesthetic form, communal signal, or first-person orientation. The framework need not force all meaningful language into one sharply bounded formal class.

## 4.4 Register inheritance

A sentence inherits the stricter standard when it performs descriptive work inside a practical argument.

Suppose a community says:

> This treatment is divinely required because it always cures the illness.

The claim about cure is descriptive. Its religious setting does not exempt it from evidence. If the factual premise fails, the action-guiding conclusion loses that support even if the community retains a symbolic or devotional interpretation of the practice.

Register inheritance prevents a mixed utterance from borrowing insulation from its commitment component while continuing to do descriptive work.

## 4.5 Not verificationism

The account does not say that only empirically testable sentences are meaningful. It says that different uses answer to different standards, and that descriptive work cannot be hidden inside a non-descriptive form.

Mathematics, logic, modality, normativity, ritual, poetry, myth, and commitment can all possess content. The formal consequence relation applies only to the addressable fragment for which stable realization conditions have been responsibly supplied.

# 5. Frege–Geach and the Boundary of the Formal Result

## 5.1 The objection

Non-cognitivist and expressivist accounts face a familiar challenge. If a sentence does not contribute stable content when embedded, how can it appear in a conditional, negation, or argument without changing meaning?

Consider:

1. If trusting God requires refusing grief, then trusting God can require psychological harm.
2. Trusting God requires refusing grief.
3. Therefore trusting God can require psychological harm.

The embedded and asserted occurrences must contribute sufficiently stable content for the inference not to equivocate.

## 5.2 What the paper assumes rather than explains

Preferential semantics begins with a language, a realization relation, and extensions. It presupposes that the formalized contents remain stable across embedding. KLM consequence does not generate compositional meaning and cannot, by itself, solve the Frege–Geach problem.

The paper therefore adopts a narrower position.

For the representable fragment, commitment contents are treated as stable, extension-like, and Boolean-composable. The two registers differ primarily in speech-act force and assessment standard, not necessarily in semantic type. A proposition-like content can be asserted as description, adopted as plan, undertaken as vow, or embedded in reasoning.

This move avoids claiming that an unstructured attitude becomes compositional merely because a preferential consequence relation is placed above it. It also concedes a familiar criticism: assigning realization extensions can look like relabeling propositions. The paper does not claim semantic novelty here.

## 5.3 What the KLM layer contributes

Given stable commitment contents, the formal layer asks a different question:

> What follows defeasibly from those contents across the best viable realizations of a fixed evaluation model?

The answer distinguishes:

- what every best realization supports;
- what at least one supports;
- what none supports;
- what cannot be realized;
- and what the model cannot yet decide.

That is a consequence architecture, not a theory of meaning.

The fixed-base control matters. A countermodel shows that premise-responsive viability revision can violate Cautious Monotony; the paired control shows that the same worlds and ordering do not violate it when premises filter one fixed base. The formal contribution lies in isolating the source of failure and preserving the distinction between model revision and inference inside a model.

### 5.3.1 Compact countermodel and fixed-base control

A three-world construction makes the distinction visible. Let

\[
W=\{w_1,w_2,w_3\},
\]

with \(w_1\vDash b\land g\), \(w_2\vDash\neg b\land g\), and \(w_3\vDash b\land\neg g\). Order \(w_1\prec w_2\) and \(w_3\prec w_2\), leaving \(w_1\) and \(w_3\) incomparable. If the viable base is allowed to change with the premises,

\[
V_{\varnothing}=\{w_1,w_2\},
\qquad
V_{\{b\}}=\{w_3\},
\]

then \(\varnothing\mid\!\sim_C b\) and \(\varnothing\mid\!\sim_C g\), but \(\{b\}\not\mid\!\sim_C g\). Cautious Monotony fails because adding \(b\) changes the viable base rather than merely filtering it.

Under the fixed-base control, one declared \(V_F\) is held constant and premises act only by intersection with \(\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma)\). The same worlds and ordering then remain within the KLM fixed-base setting. The contrast is not a claim that model revision is illegitimate; it shows that revision and inference are different operations and cannot share one theorem without further proof.

## 5.4 Object-language conditionals and metalinguistic consequence

An object-language conditional belongs to \(L_R\) and receives a realization extension through the adopted semantic clauses.

The relation:

\[
\Gamma\mid\!\sim_C\varphi
\]

is metalinguistic. It says that \(\varphi\) holds throughout the minimal \(\Gamma\)-realizing worlds of the fixed viability base. It is not itself the meaning of “if,” “ought,” “promise,” or any religious expression.

Conflating the two would make preferential consequence appear to solve a compositionality problem it presupposes away.

## 5.5 Relation to expressivist and imperative semantics

Gibbard’s planning semantics, Yalcin’s expressivist formalism, and work on imperatives show that practical or directive discourse can possess sophisticated logical structure without being modeled as ordinary descriptive assertion (Gibbard, 2003; Yalcin, 2012; Charlow, 2014a, 2014b). They also make the relabeling question unavoidable: if practical contents are represented by stable sets of worlds or plans, how far do they differ from propositions?

The present paper takes no general position capable of resolving that debate. It uses stable realization conditions for a limited practical purpose and locates its distinctive claim elsewhere: **classical or proposition-like content can be governed by nonmonotonic practical consequence rather than by one undifferentiated classical entailment relation.**

## 5.6 Scope of the result

The strongest defensible claim is:

> **For commitment contents representable in a declared Boolean-closed language with stable realization conditions, fixed-base preferential semantics supplies a defeasible consequence structure that preserves those contents through embedded inference.**

This claim concerns consequence, not the original constitution of meaning.

The result does not cover every use of:

- ritual;
- prayer;
- symbol;
- myth;
- poetry;
- first-person avowal;
- contemplative utterance;
- or embodied religious practice.

Those remain outside the formalization unless a responsible representation is supplied.

The paper therefore does not claim to solve the general Frege–Geach problem as an expressivist. It assumes stable content for a bounded fragment and develops a practical consequence architecture over it.

# 6. Disagreement and Model Relativity

## 6.1 Why disagreement appears difficult

If commitment uses are not assessed only as ordinary descriptions, what are people disagreeing about? One answer says that they merely express different attitudes. But many practical disputes are stronger than difference. People accuse each other of inconsistency, evasion, cruelty, corruption, or failure to live by their own stated commitments.

The account distinguishes several cases.

## 6.2 A taxonomy

### Different commitments, correct verdicts

Two agents may inhabit different commitment sets and face different trajectory spaces. One commitment may be admissible for one agent and unavailable to another without either verdict being mistaken.

This is difference, not necessarily disagreement.

### Shared commitments, conflicting verdicts

If two agents share the same facts, protected floors, affected scope, horizon, model, and ordering but disagree about whether the formal condition holds, at least one has misapplied the model.

This is genuine resolvable disagreement.

### Descriptive disagreement

Parties may disagree about \(F\): what happened, what a practice causes, whether testimony is reliable, or whether a risk is real. Ordinary evidential standards apply.

### Model disagreement

Parties may accept the same facts while using different causal, psychological, or social models. Resolution requires comparative explanation, prediction, intervention, or acknowledgment of uncertainty.

### Constraint disagreement

Parties may disagree about which capacities or rights function as protected floors and which tradeoffs are legitimate. This is a substantive moral or political disagreement, not a defect that logic resolves automatically.

### Ordering disagreement

Parties may rank viable trajectories differently. They must make the ranking explicit and defend its relation to agency, suffering, truth, justice, and repair.

### Horizon disagreement

A commitment can look stabilizing over a week and destructive over five years. A horizon is not a neutral technical choice. It must be justified by the phenomenon.

### Affected-agent disagreement

Parties may omit different people, communities, species, or future systems. Many apparent disagreements about “coherence” are disagreements about whose trajectory counts.

### Practical conflict

Two commitments may be separately warranted yet impossible to realize together. The result is conflict, negotiation, boundary, or institutional design—not necessarily an epistemic error.

## 6.3 Determinacy relative to a declared model

The formal relation is model-relative.

Given the same \(V_F\), \(\prec\), realization semantics, floors, affected scope, and horizon, different reasoners can reproduce the same verdict. This is determinate relative to shared public arguments.

It is not a guarantee of intersubjective objectivity across rival models. Whoever controls the viability base and ordering can control the verdicts. An institution can omit alternatives, exclude affected people, or deliberately provide a coarse model that returns no verdict.

Public specification makes those choices visible. It does not make them contestable.

Contestation requires additional normative architecture:

- affected-party standing;
- option-set integrity;
- independent challenge;
- protected dissent;
- noncompensation for severe concentrated harm;
- practical correctability;
- and authority to revise or reject the model.

The formal relation supplies none of these by itself.

## 6.4 Arbitrariness constrained, not disagreement abolished

The account constrains arbitrariness in several ways.

First, descriptive facts remain answerable to evidence regardless of preference.

Second, stable realization semantics and explicit inference constrain which conclusions follow inside a model.

Third, protected floors, affected-party scope, and governance requirements constrain local integration purchased through domination or exclusion.

Fourth, model revisions must be disclosed rather than smuggled in as premise addition.

These constraints do not guarantee one final ordering. Cross-model disagreement may remain transparent and unresolved. Naming that limit is preferable to pretending the formalism adjudicates values it only exposes.

# 7. Asymmetric Permeability

## 7.1 Description to commitment

Facts may enter commitment evaluation as constraints.

If a sacred text is shown not to be an accurate geological account, a commitment that requires treating it as geology loses warrant in that use. The community may preserve the text as myth, ritual, poetry, covenant, or theological interpretation, but it may not use the attractiveness of those functions to re-establish the descriptive claim.

If a healing practice is shown to cause harm, its devotional meaning cannot exempt it from that evidence.

If a political identity depends on a false historical claim, the identity does not manufacture the history.

## 7.2 Commitment to description

The converse evidential path is blocked.

A commitment may motivate inquiry. It may suggest hypotheses, direct attention, sustain courage, or affect theory choice where evidence underdetermines alternatives. Testimony and values can reveal neglected objects of study. None of this makes inquiry value-free.

But practical warrant does not count as evidence that a cosmology or causal claim is true. Commitments shape inquiry without deciding its descriptive results.

This distinction is the line the framework commits itself to hold as disciplinedly as possible.

## 7.3 The answer to the insulation objection

The account does not protect commitment from criticism. It relocates and pluralizes criticism.

A commitment can fail because it:

- contradicts established fact;
- lacks stable, addressable content for the claim being made;
- violates protected floors;
- requires self-deception;
- disables agency;
- externalizes costs;
- forecloses revision;
- depends on a captured model;
- or produces trajectories rejected throughout the best set of an adequately governed evaluation.

That is enough to distinguish disciplined from destructive commitment without pretending to know what cannot presently be known.

# 8. Transformative Commitment and Model Revision

## 8.1 Why instantaneous stability is too conservative

Some warranted transformations begin with disruption.

A person may accept a painful truth that their current identity cannot yet integrate. A religious practitioner may discover that a foundational historical claim is false. A patient may receive a diagnosis that reorganizes their life. A community may admit harm that destabilizes its public self-understanding.

At the moment of recognition, descriptive adequacy may rise while integration falls. If warrant requires immediate stability, the framework will counsel denial precisely when truth demands reorganization.

## 8.2 Trajectory worlds

The solution is to evaluate extended trajectories.

A trajectory may contain:

- temporary destabilization;
- grief;
- loss of role;
- a bounded decline in functioning;
- new support;
- reorganization;
- later recovery;
- newly acquired capacities;
- or foreclosed possibilities.

The fixed viability predicate evaluates the trajectory as a whole. The proposition representing adoption selects trajectories in which the commitment is adopted. It does not redefine the viability base merely by being added as a premise.

## 8.3 Bounded troughs

A transformative trajectory should be distinguished from romanticized collapse.

Responsible adoption requires attention to:

1. an inviolable viability floor;
2. the depth and duration of the trough;
3. credible recovery mechanisms;
4. evidence-sensitive expectations;
5. available lower-cost alternatives;
6. distribution of costs across affected people;
7. an expiry condition;
8. retained option value where severe harm is possible.

No commitment becomes warranted merely because suffering can be narrated as growth.

## 8.4 When the model changes

If an analyst changes protected floors, affected-agent scope, horizon, causal model, or ordering in response to a commitment, the comparison is between different models:

\[
M_0
\longrightarrow
M_1.
\]

That can be necessary. Transformative commitments may reveal that the old model omitted a person, misunderstood a capacity, or used the wrong horizon.

But the revision must be declared, dated, and justified. A verdict issued in \(M_0\) does not inherit authority in \(M_1\) without a bridge argument showing which facts, constraints, and order relations were preserved. The System P theorem does not transfer automatically to a non-intersective premise-responsive selection rule.

A commitment must not protect itself by repeatedly redefining harm, agency, affected parties, or horizon and then presenting each revision as though it preserved the warrant of the previous model.

## 8.5 Commitment-trajectory review

A commitment should be reviewed not only by what it says, but by what it becomes.

Questions include:

- What does it train the person to notice?
- What becomes difficult to acknowledge?
- Which emotions become forbidden?
- Whose testimony becomes unintelligible?
- Which authorities become immune?
- What forms of relationship are produced?
- Can doubt, grief, refusal, and revision still occur?
- What happens when the commitment acquires power?

This is the practical discipline of the commitment register.

# 9. The Two Widows Revisited

## 9.1 Scope and stipulation

The example is schematic, conditional, non-clinical, and culturally non-universal.

Quiet, delayed, private, or nonexpressive grief is not evidence of pathology or suppression. Behavioral signs alone never establish the inner organization of bereavement. The case stipulates that avoidance, brittleness, and loss of relational availability are causally linked to a rule that grief must be refused as doubt.

The formal question is therefore:

> Given the stipulated facts and declared model, how are two different expansions of one theological background evaluated?

## 9.2 Shared background

Let both women share the commitment set \(\Gamma\):

- the dead are with God;
- death is not the end;
- grief is not despair;
- trust is an appropriate response to what cannot be understood.

For the formal comparison, these items enter \(\Gamma\) as commitment contents: trust, hope, and an orientation toward grief and death. Their descriptive counterparts are not placed in the established fact set \(F\); they remain undecided. The paper therefore does not decide whether the theological descriptions are true. It asks how the same commitment contents are inhabited, and the comparison is intended to hold whatever the truth of the undecided descriptions.

Consider two candidate expansions:

- \(\varphi_s\): grief manifests insufficient trust and should be suppressed;
- \(\varphi_g\): grief is compatible with hope and may be fully undergone.

## 9.3 The declared evaluation

A responsible model must identify at least:

- the widow herself;
- close relationships and the mourning community;
- affective access and flexibility;
- factual self-contact;
- relational availability;
- ordinary functioning;
- capacity for revision;
- freedom from coercive spiritual bypassing;
- cultural context;
- and a multi-year horizon.

These choices are not dictated by logic. They are the substantive human content of the evaluation.

## 9.4 Suppression

Suppression may perform well over a short interval. It can reduce visible distress, preserve role continuity, and secure communal approval. It may therefore remain physically possible and locally stabilizing.

But the stipulated trajectory is different. Grief continues to organize avoidance outside the avowed self-understanding. The widow cannot say her husband’s name, avoids reminders, and becomes brittle toward others who mourn.

The case stipulates two insulated organizations:

- an avowed identity in which faith has absorbed the loss;
- an excluded bereavement that continues to shape conduct.

Factual self-contact degrades. Relational availability narrows. The practice becomes less capable of recognizing its own effects.

If every best viable trajectory under the declared model rejects \(\varphi_s\), then:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\bot}\varphi_s.
\]

The suppression commitment is excluded without any verdict on the afterlife.

## 9.5 Grieving with hope

The second expansion permits the loss to remain loss. Hope does not cancel grief; it changes the horizon within which grief is borne.

If at least one best viable trajectory supports this form of mourning, then:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\exists}\varphi_g.
\]

It is admissible.

If every best viable trajectory supports it relative to the declared model, then:

\[
F,\Gamma\models_C^{\forall}\varphi_g.
\]

It is strongly warranted.

The story itself does not prove either quantification. It identifies dimensions a serious model would need to represent.

## 9.6 What the case establishes

The conditional example shows four things.

First, doctrinal identity does not entail practical identity.

Second, sincerity does not guarantee warrant.

Third, a commitment can be criticized without its metaphysical picture being settled.

Fourth, spiritual hope can remain intact while a harmful interpretation of that hope is rejected.

Criticism need not choose between accepting an entire doctrinal package and destroying the practice. Register decomposition permits repair.

# 10. Deferred Inquiry Under Resource Constraint

## 10.1 The difficult case

Suppose an agent encounters credible evidence against a factual claim central to their religious or ideological community. Their livelihood, family, status, friendships, and practical identity are embedded in that community. Immediate acceptance of the evidence may not produce reorganization. It may produce collapse.

Three responses are available:

- **rationalize:** absorb the anomaly through an ad hoc explanation;
- **reorganize:** accept the evidence and rebuild the commitment structure;
- **defer:** hold the anomaly open while building the resources required for responsible inquiry.

## 10.2 Rationalization

Rationalization restores local fit by preventing evidence from altering protected identity. It changes evidential standards, invents exceptions, discredits sources selectively, or converts every counterexample into proof of persecution.

The resulting stability is purchased through degraded adequacy and foreclosed correction. It is excluded when better viable trajectories remain available.

## 10.3 Reorganization

Reorganization is often the epistemically direct response. But an agent may lack the supports required to survive it.

An account that commands immediate detonation of a person’s social and material world may confuse severity with rigor. The fact that a belief is false does not imply that every route to releasing it is equally viable.

## 10.4 Deferral

Deferral can be warranted when it means:

- not asserting the disputed claim more strongly;
- preserving the anomaly rather than explaining it away;
- seeking independent relationships, advice, and material support;
- building emotional and intellectual capacity;
- preserving a protected route of exit;
- setting an expiry or review condition;
- arranging review outside the community that benefits from continued deferral;
- and preparing for later reorganization.

Deferral ceases to be warranted when the conditions for inquiry are present, when capacity-building stops, when the expiry condition is indefinitely reset without independent review, or when delay continues to impose serious costs on others.

This is not permission to believe whatever is comforting. The descriptive claim remains constrained by evidence. The practical question is how an agent can come into truthful relation with it without avoidable collapse.

## 10.5 The community’s responsibility

A community that makes exit catastrophic manufactures the conditions under which deferral becomes rational.

Such a community degrades its own epistemic integrity by:

- raising the cost of registering disconfirmation;
- controlling relationships and resources;
- punishing inquiry;
- isolating members from alternative support;
- converting social dependence into apparent conviction;
- and certifying its own members as perpetually “not ready” to question.

The framework can therefore return paired verdicts:

- deferral may be warranted for the individual;
- the structure making deferral necessary may be unwarranted at the collective level.

This avoids blaming the agent for responding rationally to constraint while refusing to launder the institution that created it.

# 11. Political Identity and the Compassion Corollary

## 11.1 Why the political case is harder

Religious traditions can sometimes preserve a narrative as myth, covenant, ritual, or commitment after releasing a factual interpretation. Political claims often have less room for this move because they guide collective action through their descriptive content.

Claims about what happened, what a group is like, what a policy does, or which risks are real are descriptions. Their purpose is to guide action. Reclassifying them as symbolic meaning may leave them unable to perform the political work for which they were introduced.

Political identity therefore creates strong pressure toward rationalization when factual correction threatens community, status, livelihood, or belonging.

## 11.2 The symmetric diagnostic

The framework does not identify one faction as uniquely vulnerable. The process question applies symmetrically:

> Does this community preserve or foreclose the trajectories through which its members could discover that it is wrong?

A political community is epistemically degraded when it:

- makes disagreement socially catastrophic;
- supplies instant anomaly-absorbing explanations;
- applies different evidential standards to allies and opponents;
- controls the option set;
- punishes members who update;
- or erases independent records and challenge channels.

Symmetry of method does **not** imply symmetry of evidence. Equal standards applied to unequal evidence should yield unequal verdicts. That is the standards working rather than failing.

The truth of a community’s present claim does not excuse a structure incapable of future correction.

## 11.3 Evaluating claims and evaluating agents

A false claim and the person holding it require different assessments.

The claim is evaluated against descriptive evidence. The agent is evaluated relative to their trajectory space, resources, dependencies, knowledge, control, and available alternatives.

Two agents can hold the same false claim while occupying different practical positions. One may have a viable route to correction; another may face catastrophic loss with no support.

This yields the compassion corollary:

> A person may be responding to a constrained trajectory space rather than displaying stupidity, dishonesty, or bad character.

Compassion does not change the truth value of the claim. It changes what responsible response requires.

The person harmed by a false or oppressive commitment does not owe the believer education, forgiveness, reconciliation, emotional labor, or continued relationship. Compassion toward constrained agents must not become a new burden placed on those who bear the harm.

## 11.4 Changing the trajectory space

Where the binding constraint is resource scarcity rather than evidential access, more argument may address the wrong variable.

Helpful interventions can include:

- relationships outside the community;
- material and legal support;
- protected confidentiality;
- a place to belong after revision;
- slower inquiry;
- trauma-informed support;
- and examples of identity continuity after belief change.

> **[Empirical hypothesis]** When identity-relevant revision carries no viable exit, otherwise sound argument is more likely to generate resistance, concealment, or defensive entrenchment than when material, relational, and identity continuity are protected. The claim requires empirical testing and does not follow from the formal consequence relation alone.

## 11.5 Reflexive use

This analysis applies reflexively or not at all.

A person who uses the framework only to explain opponents has converted it into another identity-protective instrument. Its first use should be examination of the trajectory structures surrounding one’s own commitments.

# 12. Placement in the Philosophical Landscape

## 12.1 Kant

The title inherits Kant’s declaration that knowledge must be limited to make room for faith (Kant, 1781/1787/1998, Bxxx). The present proposal follows the limiting impulse and changes the positive term.

The aim is not to abolish knowledge. It is to deny counterfeit knowledge: claims of descriptive certainty where the available methods do not warrant it.

What enters the opened space is not immunity but commitment—orientation undertaken without pretending that orientation is evidence.

## 12.2 Wittgenstein

The early Wittgenstein supplies a discipline of boundary: where picturing fails, one should not counterfeit description. The later Wittgenstein supplies an account of use: meaning is carried by practices, inferential roles, and forms of life (Wittgenstein, 1921/2001, 1953/2009).

The present account joins these without treating practice as self-validating. Use helps identify principal function; practical warrant leaves commitments criticizable.

## 12.3 Truth-apt religious epistemology

Alston’s account of doxastic practices and Plantinga’s account of warranted Christian belief defend the rationality of explicitly truth-apt religious belief (Alston, 1991; Plantinga, 2000).

The present paper does not displace those projects. It addresses a different question: how religious and metaphysical commitments remain criticizable when their principal function in a context is practical, existential, ritual, or identity-forming rather than the assertion of an evidentially settled description.

## 12.4 Hare, Phillips, and Nielsen

Hare’s *blik* identifies world-orienting frameworks but lacks a sufficiently discriminating account of sane and insane bliks (Hare, 1955).

Phillips and Wittgensteinian philosophy of religion preserve the internal grammar of faith but remain exposed when grammar becomes insulation (Phillips, 1970).

Nielsen’s criticism is therefore decisive: a practice cannot be protected merely by declaring its standards internal (Nielsen, 1967).

The present account responds by making commitment open to factual constraint, negative verdict, model comparison, trajectory review, and external governance.

## 12.5 Carnap and Quine

Carnap’s framework questions illuminate the practical adoption of a linguistic or conceptual framework (Carnap, 1950). The two-register architecture is similarly adopted for the work it enables rather than derived from a neutral language outside all frameworks.

Quine’s holism complicates any sharp separation of isolated statements from wider webs of belief (Quine, 1951). The present account agrees that commitments and descriptions operate within networks, while retaining a functional distinction among the kinds of work particular utterances perform.

## 12.6 Expressivism, plans, imperatives, and Frege–Geach

Expressivist and plan-based accounts have developed sophisticated responses to embedding. Gibbard represents normative judgment through plans; Yalcin develops Bayesian expressivism; Charlow explores imperative content and argues that some versions of expressivism can avoid familiar Frege–Geach problems (Gibbard, 2003; Yalcin, 2012; Charlow, 2014a, 2014b). Schroeder provides a useful statement of the historical and contemporary problem (Schroeder, 2008b).

This paper does not claim priority for the idea that non-descriptive or practical discourse can possess logical structure. Nor does it claim to provide a new general semantics of that discourse.

Its specific proposal is to assume stable realization conditions for a bounded, representable commitment fragment and then use fixed-base nonmonotonic consequence to distinguish:

- support in every best realization;
- support in at least one;
- exclusion from all;
- infeasibility;
- and underdetermination.

The novelty, if the proposal is novel, lies in the combination and practical taxonomy rather than in Boolean semantics or preferential logic taken separately.

## 12.7 Brandom and inferentialism

Inferentialism explains content through the commitments and entitlements constituted by practices of giving and asking for reasons (Brandom, 1994). The present proposal shares that emphasis and adds trajectory-level practical evaluation.

The meaning of a commitment includes its inferential role; its responsible adoption also depends on what kind of life and institution that role produces.

## 12.8 Price and global expressivism

Global expressivist approaches emphasize the practical functions language serves (Price, 2011). The two-register model is less global: it preserves a robust description register while recognizing commitment force and other non-descriptive functions.

The asymmetry is deliberate. Commitment does not replace truth; it begins where truth alone does not settle how to live.

## 12.9 Moral discourse

The paper is not a general expressivism about morals.

Moral discourse includes:

- descriptions of harm and capacity;
- normative judgments;
- public commitments;
- institutional authorizations;
- and reasons governing relationship.

The Version 0.10 Ethics framework defends substantive principles concerning standing, agency, welfare, rights, distribution, power, responsibility, and repair. The two-register architecture can decompose moral utterances without reducing them all to commitment speech acts.

# 13. What Commitments This Account Licenses

> **Register note.** “Licenses” does not mean “scientifically proves” or “logically entails.” The commitments below are orientations made intelligible by the account. Facts constrain them; they do not establish facts. They remain answerable to the trajectories they generate and open to revision.

## 13.1 CU-COM1 — Commitment to Truth

The first commitment is not to a religious doctrine. It is to truth.

We commit to making reality more important than the identities, affiliations, hopes, fears, and spiritual interpretations formed around it.

This means refusing to let what we need to be true decide what we accept as fact. It also means refusing the opposite error: treating the limits of knowledge as proof that no responsible faith, metaphysical interpretation, or sacred orientation is possible.

## 13.2 CU-COM2 — Discipline of the Two Registers

We commit to holding the distinction between description and commitment as disciplinedly as possible.

We will not use a commitment's beauty, usefulness, or healing power as evidence for its descriptive cosmology.

We will not dismiss a commitment merely because it does not function as an empirical hypothesis.

We will decompose mixed utterances so that each component answers to the right standard.

## 13.3 CU-COM3 — Revision in Both Registers

Descriptions remain revisable by evidence and argument.

Commitments remain revisable when they:

- depend on false premises;
- require self-deception;
- degrade reality contact;
- foreclose grief, doubt, or inquiry;
- protect domination;
- erase affected people;
- or generate institutions incapable of correction.

No sacred commitment becomes sacred by becoming unrevisable.

## 13.4 The wider commitment horizon

The two-register discipline makes room for substantive commitment; it does not itself establish which commitments should be adopted. The Version 0.10 canonical registry now records several commitments that this paper helps make intellectually possible: **CU-COM14** on love, **CU-COM15** on meaning as discovery and participation, **CU-COM16** on freedom of meaning-revision, **CU-COM17** on redemptive possibility without justification of evil, **CU-COM18** on Heaven on Earth as social vocation, and **CU-COM19** on enacting Omega in the present.

Those commitments receive their substantive arguments in the canonical and domain papers. This companion contributes the discipline by which they can be held without being disguised as descriptive knowledge: fact constrains them; their trajectories remain assessable; and none becomes immune to revision by acquiring sacred language.

## 13.5 Faith after counterfeit knowledge

Faith can be understood as revisable trust: a way of proceeding under uncertainty without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

Such faith can orient prayer, love, service, humility, reverence, and endurance. It can receive a metaphysical picture without converting the picture into an evidential conclusion.

This is faith with standards rather than faith without risk.

## 13.6 The practice

The two-register architecture is ultimately less a doctrine than a discipline.

Before asserting, ask: **Am I describing?** If so, what evidence would count?

Before committing, ask: **What does this commitment make possible, and what does it train me not to see?**

When the two functions are mixed, separate them.

When a commitment generates harm, do not defend it merely because it once gave meaning.

When evidence destabilizes identity, build the conditions under which truth can be survived.

When another person cannot yet revise, ask whether the barrier is argument or trajectory.

And when the framework itself stops serving truth, revise the framework.

## 13.7 A concise vow

> We commit not to let our commitments decide what we believe as fact.  
> We commit not to use facts as an excuse to abandon meaning, love, or sacred responsibility.  
> We commit to assess what our commitments make of us and of the institutions we build.  
> We commit to revise descriptions when evidence requires it and commitments when truth, consequence, or compassion requires it.  
> We commit to deny counterfeit knowledge in order to make room for responsible commitment.

---

# 14. Conclusion

The philosophy of religion has often treated its central problem as a contest between evidence and faith. That framing misses an important possibility.

A stable content can be used descriptively, committally, ritually, symbolically, or in mixed form. Description remains answerable to truth and evidence. Commitment remains answerable to fact, practical warrant, agency, consequence, correction, and repair.

For the representable fragment, the paper assumes stable Boolean realization conditions. It does not derive them and does not solve the general Frege–Geach problem. Its formal contribution begins afterward: fixed-base preferential consequence provides a nonmonotonic structure for reasoning with those contents.

The resulting architecture distinguishes:

- strong warrant when every best viable realization supports a commitment;
- mere admissibility when some but not all do;
- exclusion when none do;
- infeasibility when no premise-compatible viable realization exists;
- underdetermination when the model cannot responsibly decide;
- practical expansion warrant when adoption survives the wider tests of fact, floors, affected agents, option sets, independent challenge, horizon, uncertainty, reversibility, and revision.

The two widows show why those distinctions matter when the relevant facts are stipulated rather than diagnosed. Identical doctrine can organize lives differently. One form of hope can become a prohibition on grief; another can accompany mourning and deepen compassion. The difference is not captured by truth, evidence, or sincerity alone.

The account does not prove theology. It does not reduce spiritual life to utility. It does not make coherence a universal scalar, and it does not ask logic to supply its own values. Public formalization does not prevent captured models; standing, option-set integrity, correctability, and noncompensation remain load-bearing normative requirements.

The paper’s distinctive proposal is more modest and more defensible than a general semantics of commitment:

> **Stable commitment contents can be governed by defeasible rather than classical practical consequence, allowing responsible reasoners to distinguish what every good realization supports from what merely remains possible in one.**

Kant denied knowledge to make room for faith. The present proposal denies counterfeit knowledge to make room for commitment—and then refuses to let commitment become a refuge from truth.

# Appendix A. Formal Results and Scope

## A.1 Realization semantics and fixed-base consequence

Let \(L_R\) be the Boolean-closed representable commitment fragment with realization relation \(\Vdash_R\). Let:

\[
M=(W,V_F,\prec,\Vdash_R),
\]

with \(V_F\) and \(\prec\) fixed as premises vary. Define:

\[
\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma)
=
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket_R,
\]

and:

\[
\Gamma\mid\!\sim_C\varphi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Min}_{\prec}
\left(
V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma)
\right)
\subseteq
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R.
\]

Under standard definability and smoothness conditions, the relation satisfies System P. The Or rule requires the Boolean clause:

\[
\llbracket\alpha\lor\beta\rrbracket_R
=
\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_R
\cup
\llbracket\beta\rrbracket_R.
\]

If the preference is ranked and every relevant nonempty definable set attains a minimum, the relation satisfies Rational Monotony.

The theorem concerns consequence over stable contents. It does not supply their compositional meaning.

## A.2 Model-level verdicts

Let:

\[
W_{F,\Gamma}
=
V_F\cap\operatorname{Real}_R(\Gamma),
\qquad
B(F,\Gamma)=\operatorname{Min}_{\prec}(W_{F,\Gamma}).
\]

Then:

- **strong warrant:** \(B\neq\varnothing\) and \(B\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R\);
- **mere admissibility:** \(B\neq\varnothing\), \(B\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R\neq\varnothing\), and \(B\nsubseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R\);
- **exclusion:** \(B\neq\varnothing\) and \(B\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R=\varnothing\);
- **infeasibility:** \(W_{F,\Gamma}=\varnothing\);
- **underdetermination:** \(W_{F,\Gamma}\neq\varnothing\) but no responsible best-set verdict is available because of non-attainment or model inadequacy.

In finite strict partial orders, every nonempty \(W_{F,\Gamma}\) has a minimum. The finite regression therefore tests infeasibility but not non-attainment.

## A.3 Classical-negation consequence

With:

\[
\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket_R
=
W\setminus\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_R,
\]

exclusion of \(\varphi\) is equivalent to strong warrant for \(\neg\varphi\). This is an adopted simplification. Richer distinctions among ceasing, suspending, abstaining, and non-applicability require a richer action language.

## A.4 Maximum preservation

Where a real-valued score is used and maxima exist:

\[
\max_{W_{F,\Gamma\cup\{\varphi\}}}C
\ge
\max_{W_{F,\Gamma}}C
\]

can hold only as equality because the first domain is a subset of the second. Equality is equivalent to support by at least one old maximizer. It is therefore a test of credulous admissibility, not skeptical consequence.

## A.5 Admissibility is not System P

The two-best-world construction in §3.4 shows that admissibility can fail conjunction. No System P claim is made for it.

## A.6 Trajectory lifting

Replacing instantaneous worlds with trajectories preserves the abstract fixed-base preferential construction when:

- the trajectory-level viability base remains fixed;
- the ordering remains fixed;
- and the commitment premise filters trajectories by intersection.

Changing protected floors, affected-agent scope, horizon, or ordering is model revision.

## A.7 Formal limitation and governance dependency

The formal results do not determine:

- who counts as affected;
- which floors are inviolable;
- which model is adequate;
- how uncertainty is treated;
- which options should have been considered;
- which ordering is normatively justified;
- or who possesses standing to challenge the model.

Public specification gives visibility, not contestability. Responsible use depends on affected-party standing, option-set integrity, independent challenge, practical correctability, noncompensation, and proportional reversibility safeguards.
