# Wave 5 Technical Addendum: Correctability and the Self-Deception Throughline

## Status

Cross-domain organizing interpretation and governance commentary under CU-L7. It is not a universal empirical law and does not make self-deception the cause of every failure.

## Genus and species

**Correctability** is the capacity of a model, measure, agent, or institution to remain answerable to relevant reality, affected-party evidence, consequence, challenge, and repair.

- **Motivated loss:** a protected identity, attachment, advantage, authority, objective, or policy shapes what is admitted or counted. Examples include personal self-deception, normative rationalization, silent optimization, metric capture, feedback capture, and self-certified review.
- **Non-culpable loss:** contact is lost through inherited protection, missing or delayed observation, underdetermination, limited capacity, or a model that once tracked danger and outlived its conditions.

Error, disagreement, trauma, uncertainty, scarcity, and tragedy do not by themselves establish self-deception.

## Characteristic form

A recurring failure occurs when improvement in the **reading** substitutes for improvement in the **target state**. This is diagnostic only after the represented condition, omitted effects, protected stake, and correction channels have been identified.

## Domain mappings

- Biology: regulatory misregistration and exported costs.
- Psychology: motivated self-deception versus non-culpable protective adaptation.
- Social Dynamics: metric absolutism, silent optimization, captured records, and self-certified correction.
- Ethics: rationalization and justificatory integrity, with the candid-tyrant boundary.
- AI: reward hacking, feedback capture, alignment faking, omitted groups, and provider self-certification.

## Safeguard

The throughline must never function as an accusation algorithm. Attributions require process evidence, plausible stake protection, counterfactual asymmetry, comparison with alternatives, and practical openness to correction.
